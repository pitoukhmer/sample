# Ultimate Guitar Monetizes Scale, Leaves Khmer Unserved

Ultimate Guitar (UG) is a 28-year-old, user-generated tab catalog that Muse Group has converted into a discount-driven subscription machine: roughly 60 to 77 million monthly web visits (third-party estimates), a self-reported 30 million monthly active users and 102 million app downloads, a US App Store Music ranking of #7 top-grossing against #29 top-free, and an iOS revenue estimate of about $3 million per month from Sensor Tower (unaudited), all funded by a funnel that anchors a $99 annual list price, sells it at "80% off" on a near-permanent countdown, attaches the 7-day trial to the annual plan, and auto-renews at full price. That funnel is also its biggest liability: a June 2025 federal class action, a Trustpilot score hovering between 3.2 and 3.7, and a review corpus dominated by surprise renewals, ads shown to paying subscribers, and formerly free features (transpose, Simplify, autoscroll) migrating behind the paywall. UG's catalog and licensing position (thousands of publisher deals, a 2010 Harry Fox Agency agreement, and sister company Hal Leonard since December 2023) is a genuine moat for Western repertoire, but it is thin or absent in Southeast Asian languages, where free local sites already out-rank it (Chordtela draws two to four times UG's Indonesian traffic; DoChord and ChordTabs lead Thailand; Hợp âm chuẩn owns Vietnam with a $1.99 ad-removal purchase), and Khmer has no living chord product at all: the only two dedicated apps were iOS-only, last updated February 2023, and are delisted. For a solo founder in Cambodia, the realistic play is not to out-license UG in English but to build a chords-first, offline-first, Android-and-web product for Khmer (then Thai, Vietnamese, Indonesian, Filipino) repertoire, with transparent monthly or prepaid pricing over KHQR and app-store billing, worship and beginner features UG lacks, and a legally conservative content model (chords and user-contributed charts first, licensed lyrics later). The legal and licensing material below is a synthesis of published sources and is not legal advice; prices and traffic figures are flagged as estimates wherever a primary source was not available.

## A Kaliningrad tab archive became a debt-financed Cyprus music group

Ultimate Guitar started on October 9, 1998, when economics student Eugeny Naidenov could not find a Guns N' Roses tab and posted his own; the operating company is Ultimate Guitar USA LLC in San Francisco and the site is now part of Muse Group ([Wikipedia: Ultimate Guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Guitar); [Muse Group About](https://www.mu.se/about)). Muse Group formed on April 26, 2021 as a Limassol, Cyprus holding for UG, MuseScore (acquired 2017), StaffPad and Audacity, with UG's executives moving into the same roles at the group ([Scoring Notes](https://www.scoringnotes.com/news/muse-group-formed-to-support-musescore-ultimate-guitar-acquires-audacity/); [Wikipedia: Muse Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muse_Group)). The pivotal deal was Hal Leonard, the largest sheet music publisher, which closed on December 1, 2023 with Francisco Partners funding the combination and both companies keeping their headquarters; at the time UG and MuseScore were cited at **300 million annual visitors and over 40 million accounts**, and the combined library at over 5.5 million scores, tabs, books, courses and backing tracks ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/music-creation-company-muse-group-acquires-sheet-music-publisher-hal-leonard-with-backing-from-private-equity-firm-francisco-partners); [mu.se](https://mu.se/post/hal-leonard-joins-muse-group)). Hal Leonard's own size is only available as third-party estimates, roughly 650 employees and about $300 million in annual sales (Milwaukee Magazine profile, undated, estimate) ([Milwaukee Magazine](https://www.milwaukeemag.com/inside-hal-leonard-corp-worlds-largest-sheet-music-publisher-wauwatosa)), which implies that print publishing, not UG subscriptions, is probably the largest revenue line in the group.

On June 3, 2026 Muse Group bought out Francisco Partners entirely, financed "through senior credit facilities arranged by J.P. Morgan and cash on hand"; Naidenov remains majority owner and CEO, terms were undisclosed, and the group now claims to serve "over 500 million annual musicians, learners, educators, creators, publishers and audio professionals" ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/francisco-partners-exits-its-minority-investment-in-muse-group-owner-of-audacity-hal-leonard-and-ultimate-guitar/); [mu.se](https://www.mu.se/posts/next-chapter-francisco-partners-conclusion)). Two inferences follow. A debt-financed buyout implies the group throws off enough cash to service bank credit, so UG is almost certainly profitable; it also adds interest burden and removes the PE exit clock, which is consistent with continued pressure to raise average revenue per user through paywalling and bundles. The 500 million figure is a cross-brand reach number (it grew from 300 million in 2023 and 400 million in mid-2025 as Hal Leonard and Audio.com were added), not a UG metric ([Cyprus Mail](https://cyprus-mail.com/2025/07/01/limassol-tech-company-bridges-global-innovation-and-local-talent)). Headcount for the whole group is only available as estimates in the 200 to 420 range ([Revelio Labs](https://www.reveliolabs.com/companies/muse-group/employees/)), and the period also saw senior MuseScore departures, which Scoring Notes links to the exit ([Scoring Notes](https://www.scoringnotes.com/news/private-equity-exits-muse-group-amidst-musescore-studio-leadership-changes/)).

UG's own scale claims are **2.1 million-plus tabs, 30 million monthly active users and 102 million app downloads** on an undated Muse Group page ([mu.se/ultimate-guitar](https://www.mu.se/ultimate-guitar)); the "200M musicians" and "53M downloads" lines seen in UG's ad creative appear in no indexed source and should be treated as unverified marketing copy. Third-party web traffic estimates disagree by tool but agree on order of magnitude: Semrush shows **61 to 77 million visits per month** across June 2025 to August 2026 (estimate), with Google organic at about 50% and the US at 22 to 26% of traffic, while Similarweb's August 2026 page shows 63.9 million visits over three months with organic search at 64% of desktop visits (estimate) ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/ultimate-guitar.com/); [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/ultimate-guitar.com/)). The country mix is the detail that matters for a Southeast Asian founder: one Semrush snapshot puts Indonesia at 11.4% (about 7.5 million visits, 84% mobile) and the Philippines at 7.9% (about 5.2 million, 67% mobile), with Thailand and Vietnam outside the top five (estimate) ([Semrush](https://cw.semrush.com/website/ultimate-guitar.com/overview)), and Ahrefs rates Indonesia as UG's single largest search market at 8.4 million monthly search visits (estimate) ([Ahrefstop](https://ahrefstop.com/websites/ultimate-guitar.com)). On mobile, the iOS app carries about 443,000 to 517,000 ratings at 4.7 to 4.8 stars, ranks #29 top-free and **#7 top-grossing in US Music** as of March 12, 2026, and Google Play shows 10 million-plus installs ([App Pricing Lab](https://apppricinglab.com/app/apple/357828853); [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/357828853); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ultimateguitar.tabs)). A top-grossing rank far ahead of the top-free rank is the signature of a mature, high-ARPU subscriber base.

Revenue is where every source conflicts, and all of it is unaudited. Sensor Tower's US iOS page (snapshot around March 2026) shows roughly **200,000 downloads and $3 million revenue in the latest month** (estimate); an Android tracker shows about $900,000 per month (estimate); AppGoblin's "$100K+ per month, 68% ads" figure is an obvious undercount given the top-grossing rank; and company databases put Ultimate Guitar USA anywhere from $2.9 million to $16.8 million per year and all of Muse Group at $15 to $25 million, which is implausibly low for a group that owns Hal Leonard ([Sensor Tower](https://app.sensortower.com/overview/357828853?country=US); [TrendApps](https://trendapps.dev/app/android/com-ultimateguitar-tabs/); [ZoomInfo](https://www.zoominfo.com/c/ultimate-guitar/351368118); [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/company/mu.se/)). Annualizing the two app-tracker snapshots gives a mobile in-app run rate on the order of $45 million per year (writer's arithmetic from single-month estimates, upper bound), before web subscriptions and advertising. Web is probably the larger subscription channel, because UG deliberately routes subscriptions through its own Paddle checkout to avoid the 15 to 30% store commission, and the $99 list price with 80% off theatre lives on the web rather than in the stores ([Paddle](https://www.paddle.com/studios/shows/fix-that-funnel/ultimate-guitar)). The honest summary is that UG's subscription business is likely in the tens of millions of dollars per year, and nobody outside Muse Group knows the number.

## Free users get the catalog; Pro gets the licensed layer and every tool

UG runs a two-track catalog. Non-official tabs are submitted by users and reviewed by volunteer and staff moderators, typically approved within 48 hours with a three-day revision window; Official tabs are made by the UG team, licensed, and carry backing tracks and synced lyrics ([UG Help: Tabs](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/6741306-tabs); [UG Help: approval time](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/6749103-how-long-does-it-usually-take-for-my-tab-to-be-approved)). The storefronts advertise 1.4 million chords, tabs and lyrics, "over 22,500" or "over 29,000" Official tabs depending on the storefront, 29,000-plus Tonebridge tone presets, Courses with 1,500 to 2,500 lessons (UG's own help pages give both figures), and Sing with 20,000-plus interactive songs ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/apple-store/id357828853); [UG Help: Courses](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/6741783-what-are-ultimate-guitar-courses)). UG never publishes a side-by-side tier matrix, which is itself a finding; the table below is reconstructed from help-center articles, store listings and reviews.

Sources for the matrix: the Pro help article lists official tabs, auto-scroll and SmartScroll, transpose, chord library and progressions, and a beginner course ([UG Help: What is Pro](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/6741560-what-is-ultimate-guitar-pro-subscription)); backing tracks are "available exclusively for Official and Pro tabs" and audio transposition shifts playback in semitone steps ([UG Help: Tab tools](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/14880177-what-are-tab-tools-and-how-to-use-them)); Official tabs cannot be downloaded and Pro tabs download only on the website ([UG Help: download and print](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/6735529-website-how-to-download-and-print-tabs)); Practice and Sing are "provided in the app only" and Courses is a separate subscription under one account ([UG Help: Guide to premium services](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/6741293-guide-to-ultimate-guitar-s-premium-services)); the free-feature migration claims come from reviewers ("They've moved key functions, Simplify and Transpose, behind a paywall. These were always free") ([App Store ZA reviews](https://apps.apple.com/za/app/id357828853); [JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/357828853/ultimate-guitar-chords-tabs/reviews)).

The AI layer is newer and narrower than the marketing suggests. Practice Mode launched January 31, 2024 as a machine-learning tool that records the session, then gives post-practice feedback on pitch and rhythm; at launch it was iOS-only, Pro or Pro+ only, covered about 23,000 Official tabs in tab and notation views, and promised chord-view support "coming soon" ([mu.se: Practice Mode launch](https://www.mu.se/posts/ultimate-guitar-launches-practice-mode)). As of the current help pages it remains app-only, requires an active Pro subscription, and is "currently not available for Official Tabs on Android devices" ([UG Help: Practice](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/14588372-what-is-ultimate-guitar-practice-subscription)). The feature is better understood as note and chord recognition applied to notation UG already owns (the same audio-recognition stack Muse Group reuses in StaffPad's Piano Capture and MuseScore's August 2026 Audio-to-Score), not free-form song detection; that is why it is limited to Official tabs and lags on Android. A third-party privacy reading notes that Practice Mode audio is used to train UG's AI, discarded within a day, with no separate opt-out described, and gives the app its lowest trust grade ([Gen Digital Agent Trust Hub](https://ai.gendigital.com/app/ultimate-guitar-com)). In May 2026 UG added "AI chords": paste a YouTube link (15 minutes maximum) under My Tabs, get several chord-chart variations "in 10 minutes or less," and rate them with thumbs up or down ([UG Help: AI chords](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/14771579-mobile-what-are-the-ai-chords)). That is UG conceding the Chordify and Chord ai use case, three years late and gated behind Pro.

The mechanism bridging the $99 list and the $20 to $40 realized price is a perpetual discount: UG's own pages carry "80% OFF" banners under rotating Summer Sale, Spring Sale and Black Friday labels, a third-party reviewer reports the promotion runs "almost every other week," and a 2021 commenter called the 80% discount "semi-permanent" ([tp.ultimate-guitar.com sitemap](https://tp.ultimate-guitar.com/sitemap.xml); [ScreenWise](https://screenwiseapp.com/media/ultimate-guitar-app); [Killer Guitar Rigs](https://killerguitarrigs.com/ultimate-guitar-pro-review/)). In January 2025 existing annual subscribers received increase notices (UK £19.99 to £29.99 from February; Australian renewals A$25 to A$30, one "special" rising to A$49.99), and forum members described the options as "designed to confuse" with "special price" offers that matched or exceeded the standard price ([JustinGuitar community](https://community.justinguitar.com/t/ultimate-guitar-price-increase/386865)). Muse Group advertises a Head of Monetization role, so this is a managed pricing function, not an accident ([Francisco Partners careers](https://careers.franciscopartners.com/companies/muse-group-2-d371613d-bc8d-47cd-b632-5eda45332da3/jobs/45340992-head-of-monetization)).

The onboarding is built for hard-paywall economics on top of a freemium catalog. A teardown counts **14 onboarding steps** (instrument, genres, age) that land directly on a three-tier paywall with a discounted "Welcome offer," monthly and yearly plans and a 7-day free trial; the same teardown reports that dismissing the paywall triggers a secondary "40% off forever" offer, a detail that could not be re-confirmed and should be treated as plausible but unverified ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/apps/ultimate-guitar-chords-tabs/)). TechRadar's reviewer found the week-long trial "shadily attached to the expensive yearly subscription" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/websites-apps/ultimate-guitar)), and a reviewer reports that after a week of inactivity the app forces the user through "multiple pages that entice you to pay" before search works again ([JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/357828853/ultimate-guitar-chords-tabs/reviews)). Cancellation is self-serve but must reach a final confirmation step or "the subscription will remain active and auto-renewal will continue," a paused subscription renews automatically after the pause, cancelling a trial ends access immediately, and "canceling your subscription does not automatically issue a refund" ([UG Help: cancel](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/6741667-how-to-cancel-your-ultimate-guitar-subscription); [UG Help: refunds](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/6741748-requesting-a-refund-for-an-ultimate-guitar-subscription-a-complete-guide)). The fact pattern is now in court: **Teilmann et al. v. Ultimate Guitar USA LLC, 25-cv-5310 (N.D. Cal., June 2025)** alleges failure to adequately disclose that free-trial users are automatically enrolled in subscriptions and that cancellation is made difficult; the tracker lists it as pending ([Truth in Advertising](https://truthinadvertising.org/class-action/ultimate-guitar/)).

Benchmarks explain why UG tolerates the reputational cost. RevenueCat's 2026 data put median Day-35 trial-to-paid at **10.7% for hard-paywall apps versus 2.1% for freemium**, with near-identical year-one retention (27% versus 28%) and roughly 8x the Day-60 revenue per install ([RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). Education, the closest proxy category, has the lowest Day-0 conversion of any category (28.5%) but above-median revenue per install ($0.30 at Day 14 versus $0.23), a 12-month LTV of about $45.10, a 50% LTV premium for trial starters, and the highest refund rate on annual plans (4.2%) ([RevenueCat 2026 Education](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education); [Adapty Education benchmarks](https://adapty.io/blog/markdown/education-app-subscription-benchmarks.md)). Annual plans see 35% of their cancellations in the first month and a second spike at month 12, and 17-to-32-day trials convert at a median 42.5% against 25.5% for trials under four days, yet nearly half of apps now run trials of four days or fewer ([RevenueCat benchmarks blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)). UG's 7-day trial tied to annual, countdown pricing and no automatic refund is therefore category-typical behavior optimized for revenue per install, and the complaint corpus is the predictable byproduct. Against the learning apps, UG's realized $20 to $40 per year sits well below Yousician Premium (about $89.99 per year), Simply Guitar (about $119.99 per year, no trial, no refunds), and Fender Play ($149.99 per year), all observed in 2026 reviews and subject to change ([Equipboard](https://equipboard.com/posts/fender-play-vs-yousician); [GuitarStartGuide](https://www.guitarstartguide.com/simply-guitar-cost/); [Guitar Chalk Fender Play](https://www.guitarchalk.com/fender-play-review/)).

## Growth runs on two million song pages and a top-grossing app slot

UG's acquisition engine is organic search on "song title + chords" queries. Semrush attributes about 50% of traffic to Google organic and Similarweb about 64% of desktop visits; the site ranks for roughly 295,000 (Similarweb) to 670,000 (Ahrefs) keywords, with top terms "ultimate guitar," "wonderwall chords," "creep chords," "hotel california chords" and "guitar tabs," and Semrush gives it an authority score of 83 ([Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/ultimate-guitar.com/); [Ahrefstop](https://ahrefstop.com/websites/ultimate-guitar.com); [Semrush](https://pl.semrush.com/website/ultimate-guitar.com/competitors)). The moat is simply the number of long-tail landing pages: two million user tabs, each a page, indexed across tabs.ultimate-guitar.com and localized subdomains ([Similarweb tabs subdomain](https://www.similarweb.com/website/tabs.ultimate-guitar.com/)). That same long tail is why Indonesia and the Philippines rank as top-three traffic sources: large English-reading, low-ARPU audiences arrive on free pages and monetize mostly through ads.

The second loop is the app store. Beyond the rankings above, UG was an early Android Instant App adopter; its React Native build exceeded the standard 4MB limit and needed Google's 10MB program, and the "Try now" path lifted install rate by 8% ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/stories/instant-apps/ultimate-guitar)). Paid acquisition is conventional performance marketing: an Admitad CPI offer for the Android app in "many GEOs" with creative rules requiring real app UI, and a CPA offer on WOWTRK ([affi.io](https://affi.io/m/ultimate-guitar-many-geos-android); [WOWTRK](https://www.wowtrk.com/offer/2892)). No consumer referral program was found anywhere, and coupon aggregators note UG "very rarely issues promotional discount codes" because discounts are site-run sales ([Dealspotr](https://dealspotr.com/promo-codes/ultimate-guitar.com)). The retention loop is Shots, an in-app short-video "open mic" reachable from the center nav icon, official tabs and profiles, with weekly #featureme features; critics call it pixelated and "another algorithm-driven feed" ([UG Help: Shots](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/6741295-ug-shots); [Screenwise](https://screenwiseapp.com/media/ultimate-guitar-app)). Display advertising to free users is sold through a media kit ([UG Advertising](https://www.ultimate-guitar.com/about/advertise.htm)).

## Licensing is the moat, and also the reason the free tier keeps shrinking

UG's rights position was built in two phases. After MXtabs.net and others shut down under Music Publishers Association pressure in late 2004 and early 2005, UG absorbed their users while arguing it sat outside MPA reach because it was headquartered in Russia; it then signed license agreements with "thousands of publishers, including Sony, EMI, Peermusic, Alfred, Hal Leonard, Faber and Music Sales," under which songwriters are paid for tab display, and on April 10, 2010 added a Harry Fox Agency agreement covering lyrics display, title search, and tab display with download and print ([Wikipedia: Ultimate Guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Guitar)). UG's own explainer says publishers "issue the licenses and collect the money" and that the HFA deal lets "tens of thousands of other publishers" opt in ([UG Blog: licensing](https://www.ultimate-guitar.com/article/blog/licensing)). No source describes royalty rates or payout totals, and no LyricFind, Musixmatch or Musicnotes relationship was found. Since December 2023 Hal Leonard is a sister company and Muse said its licensed arrangements would be "offered in digitized, interactive formats" on Muse platforms ([mu.se](https://mu.se/post/hal-leonard-joins-muse-group)); no dated announcement of Hal Leonard content actually shipping inside UG was found, so that integration remains intent rather than evidence.

The business consequence shows up in user forums. Members report that "the good tabs are being changed to 'pro' status," that "half the tabs are gone, I assume updated to pro status," and that new songbooks "took certain tabs down and locked them behind another paywall," with anything available through the Sheet Happens publisher removed even when a user tab predated the official version ([UG Forum](https://www.ultimate-guitar.com/forum/showthread.php?t=2114822); [SevenString](https://sevenstring.org/threads/ultimate-guitar-is-dead.368845/)). UG's public rationale, in a developer reply on the App Store, is that "the tabs in our database are legal and licensed so we pay royalties to copyright owners. That's why we can't make it entirely free" ([App Store NO developer response](https://apps.apple.com/no/app/id357828853)). Read together, the pattern is a deliberate strategy: let user-generated pages capture search demand for free, then progressively replace or gate the most-searched songs with staff-made, licensed versions whose royalty cost must be recovered through Pro. Publisher exclusivity terms, not accidents, explain why user tabs vanish when an official one appears. This is the structural trap a new entrant can avoid in markets where UG holds no licensed catalog, but it is also a warning: a product that hosts lyrics for Western repertoire will eventually face the same cost line.

## Complaints cluster on billing, ads for subscribers, and paywall creep

The review corpus is large and consistent. Trustpilot shows roughly 1,900 to 2,000 reviews at a TrustScore between 3.2 and 3.7 ("Average"), with UG replying to 98 to 100% of negative reviews, usually within about 14 hours; Trustpilot's summary names "confusing promotional offers and unexpected charges for multiple subscriptions," "occasional inaccuracies in tabs" and "frustration with ads, even for paying subscribers" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.ultimate-guitar.com)). unitQ scores the app 46 out of 100 ("poor") with **61% of its 673 complaint reports coming from Android** ([unitQ](https://unitq.com/unitq-scorecards/ultimateguitar)), and an AI-generated aggregator estimates about 20% of users complain about the frequency of upgrade interruptions and about 15% report billing or cancellation problems (both methodology-unknown estimates) ([Clarity](https://www.onclarity.com/leaderboard/insight/ultimateguitar)). A July 2024 r/Guitar thread titled "Ultimate Guitar is trash" drew about 1,700 upvotes, with commenters moving to Songsterr ([r/Guitar mirror](https://lr.sudovanilla.org/r/Guitar/comments/1eb516o/ultimate_guitar_is_trash/leq9u7d)).

Billing is the most common one-star theme: a 13-year-old signed up for a 7-day trial and was charged an annual fee with "no way to even get in without signing up for 7 day trial"; a reviewer who "cancelled a free trial after the FIRST day" was charged $99; another was offered "a $24 reduction after being charged $99 during a trial"; reviewers report card details they "absolutely do not allow you to delete," a promised pre-renewal reminder that "never arrived," and UG's standard reply that "the renewal was not canceled before, so you were charged for another year as advertised" ([Trustpilot](https://pt.trustpilot.com/review/www.ultimate-guitar.com); [Trustpilot page 8](https://pt.trustpilot.com/review/www.ultimate-guitar.com?page=8)). A structural cause UG documents itself: subscriptions bought while logged out attach to the device rather than the account, generating "paid but can't log in" tickets, and cancellation must happen on the platform of purchase ([UG Help iOS](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/6741380-ios-i-ve-paid-for-your-app-but-don-t-remember-the-username-and-can-t-log-in); [UG Help: cancel](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/6741667-how-to-cancel-subscription)).

Ad and upsell pressure comes second, and it hits paying users. Free users describe "video promotions for the subscription that force you to watch them for like 10 seconds before you can access or leave a tab page" and an app "pretty much unusable now with how many ads they've put in"; a paying iOS subscriber reports "aggressive ad walls every time I try and do anything"; a blind user says the Pro upsell "takes a lot of time and effort to even get out of the full version screen" ([grand-screen](https://grand-screen.com/apps/ultimate-guitar-chords-tabs/reviews/); [JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/357828853/ultimate-guitar-chords-tabs/reviews); [AppleVis](https://applevis.com/comment/83360)). Paywall creep is third: transpose, Simplify, autoscroll, printing and whole tabs reported as once free and now gated, a German reviewer whose "entire collection is gone" after an update, and a Google Play reviewer who sees the app "slowly becoming more and more like a subscription service that just offers a free trial" ([App Store ZA](https://apps.apple.com/za/app/id357828853); [grand-screen](https://grand-screen.com/apps/ultimate-guitar-chords-tabs/reviews/)). Quality and UX complaints round it out: forced "20 questions" onboarding on Android, a "blank white screen of death" on iOS, a Safari autoscroll bug at any zoom other than 100% that UG's developers already knew about, Pro tab playback that falls "out of sync pretty fast," a phone interface that "can feel a touch crowded" with small text and "too many buttons," backing tracks that cannot be downloaded for offline use, offline access limited to pre-downloaded favorites that must be re-downloaded after reinstall, and VoiceOver accessibility that regressed from "about 95% accessible" in the iOS 9 era to "more inaccessible now" with unlabeled buttons ([unitQ](https://unitq.com/unitq-scorecards/ultimateguitar); [App Store MY](https://apps.apple.com/my/app/id357828853); [WebKit Bugzilla](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=281786); [BassBuzz](https://forum.bassbuzz.com/t/syncing-actual-songs-with-ultimate-guitar/57559); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/websites-apps/ultimate-guitar); [UG Help: offline](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/7936845-mobile-offline-access-to-your-favorites-how-to-add-load-and-troubleshoot); [AppleVis](https://applevis.com/comment/83360)).

The unmet needs are just as legible. Beginners say other sources "only show when the chord changes, not the bars between" and that strumming patterns "seem to be only occasionally given," while UG pages carry the stock placeholder "there is no strumming pattern for this song yet" ([JustinGuitar community](https://community.justinguitar.com/t/strumming-patterns/79747); [UG tab](https://tabs.ultimate-guitar.com/tab/maryzark/hindi-na-chords-498596)). Amateurs want simplified "basic chord" versions, which is exactly Chordtela's positioning in Indonesia ([Mojok](https://mojok.co/terminal/chordtela-adalah-solusi-bagi-orang-yang-skill-gitarannya-pas-pasan-kayak-saya/)). Worship bands bolt a browser extension onto UG pages to get Nashville numbers because UG has none, and run Sunday on OnSong, Planning Center Music Stand or free WorshipTools instead ([KANTAN Chord](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/android/addon/instachordizer/); [learnofchrist OnSong](https://learnofchrist.com/resources/onsong); [learnofchrist WorshipTools](https://learnofchrist.com/resources/worshiptools)). Export is poor (most users copy text and hand-add section tags; iPad share yields .txt not ChordPro) ([MobileSheets forum](https://www.zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/forum/showthread.php?tid=8289)). Teachers have had no assignment-and-practice-tracking tool since Tonara shut down on December 8, 2023 ([Wikipedia: Tonara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonara_(company))).

## Chords are safe, lyrics need a license, tabs sit in untested territory

Nothing in this section is formal legal advice; it summarizes published sources and the reader should consult counsel in each jurisdiction before launch. The settled part is lyrics: they are protected literary content and require a publisher or aggregator license to display, and Google itself says it licenses lyrics from third parties rather than scraping ([9to5Google 2020](https://9to5google.com/2020/08/11/google-genius-lyric-lawsuit/)). In Genius v. Google the Second Circuit treated Genius's lyric transcriptions as derivative works of the songwriter's copyright and found Genius lacked standing because it did not own the lyrics ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/judge-throws-out-genius-lawsuit-accusing-google-of-copying-lyrics/)). The other settled-ish part is bare chord progressions, which Chordify (in 2014) and general songwriting-law commentary treat as "not innovative and sufficiently unique to be copyrighted on their own," while melody, lyrics and a written arrangement are protected ([Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2014/03/10/chordify/); [Secrets of Songwriting](https://www.secretsofsongwriting.com/2009/01/09/what-does-copyright-really-protect/)).

Tabs and chord-plus-lyric sheets are the contested middle. In 2006 the MPA and NMPA argued that tabs, "even inaccurate ones," are protected derivative works under the exclusive right to make "arrangements, adaptations, abridgements, or transcriptions," and sites took their tabs down "voluntarily, but grudgingly"; the On-line Guitar Archive removed roughly 34,000 tablatures after a takedown letter ([Arizona Law Review](https://arizonalawreview.org/pdf/50-2/50arizlrev611.pdf); [Wikipedia: OLGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-line_Guitar_Archive)). Public Knowledge judged the publishers' derivative-work argument "probably correct" and fair use "unlikely" for commercial tab sites, a Vanderbilt note concluded tabs are infringing derivative works yet protected by fair use while noting "a lack of legal precedent," and no US court has squarely ruled on tabs as of the sources found ([Public Knowledge](https://publicknowledge.org/?p=7791); [Vanderbilt JETLaw](https://scholarship.law.vanderbilt.edu/jetlaw/vol9/iss3/8)). The practical reading is a risk gradient: chords only, no lyrics, is the lowest-risk zero-license product (Chordify's original model); chords with lyric lines is where a publisher license becomes necessary; note-for-note tab in Guitar Pro style is the riskiest unlicensed category because it reproduces melody and arrangement and was exactly what the 2006 letters targeted. Hosting user-generated charts under US DMCA safe harbor requires designating an agent with the Copyright Office ($6 per filing, renewed every three years, with a lapse ending protection until renewal) and a genuine repeat-infringer policy; it covers US liability only ([US Copyright Office](https://copyright.gov/newsnet/2016/640.html); [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/want-to-keep-your-dmca-safe-harbor-new-76629/)).

Licensed lyrics are affordable per view and expensive at the door. The only concrete figures found are a 2015 Musixmatch statement that data licenses start at a **USD 25,000 annual minimum** and web or mobile display is charged at "not less than 1.4 USD CPM" per thousand lyric views; LyricFind says pricing is usage-based with royalties paid per view and requires direct contact ([Audacious Redmine issue](https://redmine.audacious-media-player.org/issues/502); [LyricFind press clipping](https://lyricfind.rockpaperscissors.biz/artist/8830/press_clips/10258/white)). These are decade-old floor estimates; no 2024 to 2026 pricing is public. At $1.40 CPM, one million monthly lyric views would cost about $1,400 per month, so the binding constraint is the minimum and the sales process, not the rate. Whether either aggregator licenses a Cambodia-registered entity, and whether their Khmer, Thai or Vietnamese coverage is adequate, is unknown; for local-language repertoire there is no LyricFind equivalent, and direct deals with local labels (which in SEA often own both master and publishing) are the realistic route. A licensed chords-plus-lyrics app, Jamzone, notes that "certain right holders can withdraw consent and we are required to not showcase chords and lyrics for some artists," so even licensed catalogs have holes ([Jamzone](https://jamzone.substack.com/p/pro-guide-recording-exporting-and/comments)).

Audio features carry their own rules. YouTube's API terms permit the IFrame embedded player with restrictions on overlays, player modification and autoplay, prohibit extracting audio, and a law-firm summary warns that sites that "simply embed a large number of YouTube videos and aim solely to earn advertising revenue" violate the terms ([Google Developers](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/required-minimum-functionality); [Monolith Law Office](https://monolith.law/en/internet/youtube-terms-of-service-important-points)). A chord-sync feature should therefore poll the player's current time and highlight chords beside the player, never touch the stream. Backing tracks of commercial songs need mechanical and master licenses, which is why most startups avoid them. Thailand's Department of Intellectual Property warned in August 2026 that using protected songs with AI may require licenses from multiple rights holders ([Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/business/tech/40069742)), while Vietnam's 2022 IP amendments explicitly permit "temporary reproductions of works during technological processes" ([Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/trademark/1498960/vietnam-ip-law-amendments-2022-what-businesses-need-to-know)); a defensible design for user-uploaded audio analysis is in-memory processing, deletion after analysis, chords-only output and a user warranty of rights.

Cambodia's framework is young and tightening. The 2003 Law on Copyright and Related Rights governs; Cambodia's Berne accession entered into force on **March 9, 2022**, so foreign works are now protected there and "hosted in Cambodia" is not the shield "hosted in Russia" was for UG in 2005 ([WIPO Berne Notification 287](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/es/treaties/notifications/details/treaty_berne_287); [Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/cambodia-accede-berne-convention-greatly-expanding-foreign-copyright-owners-rights/?page=2)). A 2016 Prakas created the legal basis for collective management organizations, but no operating Cambodian music CMO could be identified by name; the Prime Minister asked the Ministry of Culture to strengthen IP enforcement in March 2024, and amendments to the copyright law were in consultation in September 2025 ([WIPO Lex Prakas 112](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/544320); [Asia IP Law](https://asiaiplaw.com/section/cover-story/cambodia-pm-urges-ministry-to-fortify-efforts-to-ip-rights); [EU IP Helpdesk](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/ministry-culture-gathers-input-copyright-law-amendments-2025-09-20_en)). Regionally, FILSCAP, WAMI, MACP, MCT and VCPMC formed AAMRO with SESAC and MINT in July 2023 for multi-territory digital licensing, but none of the sources describe a lyric-display tariff from any of them, so a local-repertoire lyrics license will be a bespoke negotiation ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/sesac-partners-with-five-southeast-asian-collection-societies-to-form-multi-territorial-digital-licensing-hub/)). Takedown regimes differ: Thailand's 2022 amendment created a DMCA-style notice-and-takedown with hosting-provider duties; the Philippines relies on IPOPHL administrative site blocking (in effect January 14, 2024) and platform liability under the Internet Transactions Act; Indonesia's Constitutional Court Decision 84/PUU-XXI/2023 extended liability to UGC platforms; Vietnam uses blocking orders ([Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/the-2022-amendment-of-thailands-copyright-act-the-good-the-bad-and-the-backstory); [Rouse](https://rouse.com/insights/news/2024/content-and-site-blocking-in-se-asia); [Mondaq Indonesia](https://www.mondaq.com/copyright/1806078/indonesia-updates-copyright-law)). The enforcement that actually bites a small app is not a Cambodian court; it is the app stores, Google Search and payment processors, which apply their own takedown rules regardless of where the server sits.

## Store fees fell to 10 to 15%, but KHQR cannot auto-renew

Platform economics improved for small developers in 2026, with caveats. Apple's Small Business Program charges 15% on proceeds up to USD 1 million and 10% on subscriptions after their first year ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)). In the US, after the April 2025 contempt ruling, Apple may not currently collect commission on external-link purchases; on August 13, 2026 it proposed 15% standard, 10% for subscription renewals and partner programs, and 5% for Small Business Program members on linked-out purchases, which Epic is contesting and which was not final as of October 2026 ([Channel Insider](https://www.channelinsider.com/apple/news-apple-external-purchase-fee-epic-court-fight/)). Google Play, following its Epic settlement, began rolling out on June 30, 2026 in the US, UK and EEA a flat **10% service fee on the first USD 1 million per year and on auto-renewing subscriptions, plus a 5% billing fee only when Google Play Billing is used**; alternative billing or external links avoid the billing fee but not the service fee, and sources conflict on the tier above $1 million (15 to 25%); global expansion runs through September 2027 ([Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/google-starts-cutting-play-store-fees-to-a-flat-10-service-fee-on-the-first-1m-and-allows-external-payment-links-making-good-on-its-epic-games-settlement/); [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/03/05/google-play-store-changes-android-app-distribution/)). Web checkout costs about 3.6 to 5% all-in on Stripe (2.9% plus 30 cents, Billing 0.7%, Tax 0.5%, plus about 2.5% for non-US cards and currency conversion), and RevenueCat charges about 1% of tracked revenue after $2,500 per month to unify entitlements across web and stores ([Flexprice](https://flexprice.io/blog/stripe-pricing-breakdown-2026); [CostBench](https://costbench.com/compare/revenuecat-vs-stripe-billing/)). Outside the US, EU, UK, Japan and Korea, which covers all of Southeast Asia, Apple's anti-steering rules still apply, so the app should not link to web checkout in-app there; it should rely on web sign-ups and in-app login instead.

The Cambodian rails are the real constraint. ABA PayWay's KHQR codes carry an expiry and "can be paid multiple times," merchants receive webhook notifications and can issue payment links with count limits, and the Bakong Open API covers token requests, deep links and status checks; but no source shows merchant-initiated recurring debits or card-on-file tokenization, and one integrator lists ABA Payments with "Recurring support: not supported" ([PayWay KHQR guideline](https://developer.payway.com.kh/khqr-guideline-3192101f0.md); [aba-payway-sdk](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/aba-payway-sdk@0.2.35/README.md); [CartDNA](https://www.cartdna.com/en_GB/shopify-payment-methods/aba-payments)). A 2022 explainer states "Stripe is not currently available in Cambodia" and that a local merchant account requires a Cambodian company; this was not verified against Stripe for 2026, and a UK-entity workaround is marketed by a formation service ([Cambodia Investment Review](https://cambodiainvestmentreview.com/2022/06/14/explainer-how-to-receive-money-in-cambodia/); [Incorpuk](https://incorpuk.com/blog/open-stripe-account-in-cambodia/)). The workable design is therefore a three-rail model: app-store subscriptions through RevenueCat as the only native auto-renew for mobile users; KHQR treated as a pay-per-period rail with dynamic QR per cycle, webhook confirmation, reminders three days before expiry, and 3, 6 and 12-month prepay discounts; and a foreign entity on Stripe for card auto-renew in the global English market. For the wider region, Xendit documents API subscriptions with instantly activated autodebit for Maya and GrabPay in the Philippines (ShopeePay on request, GCash not listed), and PayMongo offers a Subscriptions API; MoMo and PromptPay recurring were not documented in the sources ([Xendit help](https://help.xendit.co/hc/en-us/articles/4408498827545--PH-How-to-activate-subscription-payments-for-my-business); [PayMongo](https://paymongo.com/solution/utilities)). The payment reality points in the same direction as the complaint corpus: in Cambodia a subscription app cannot practically run UG's "trial, auto-renew, refuse refund" playbook, which happens to be the thing UG's users hate most.

## The open-source stack covers every layer except reliable chord recognition

A solo founder can assemble the product from mature components. ChordSheetJS parses ChordPro and plain text, formats HTML, transposes, and converts chords to Nashville numbers; SVGuitar (MIT) renders SVG chord diagrams with barres, fingers, left-handed mode and arbitrary string counts, in browser or headless; alphaTab (MPL-2.0) renders and plays Guitar Pro 3 to 7, MusicXML and alphaTex with a built-in SoundFont synthesizer across browser, Node, .NET and Android ([ChordSheetJS](https://github.com/bettermusic/ChordSheetJS); [SVGuitar](https://github.com/omnibrain/svguitar); [alphaTab](https://github.com/CoderLine/alphaTab)). On the app side, Expo SDK 57 with React Native 0.86 shipped June 30, 2026 with zero breaking changes, and RevenueCat's own guide recommends React Native plus Expo for TypeScript developers with Supabase, EAS and Sentry, while noting that RevenueCat's 2026 dataset shows the top quartile of subscription apps growing 80% year on year and the bottom quartile shrinking 33% ([pasqualepillitteri.it](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/8279/react-native-2026-stack-choices-vs-flutter); [Expo blog](https://expo.dev/blog/best-way-to-build-a-mobile-app-that-makes-money-in-2026)). The sensible data model is ChordPro as canonical source with transposition applied client-side, chord diagrams generated from a name-to-voicing table, autoscroll as a trivial animation loop keyed to BPM, and the entire catalog synced for offline use, the Hợp âm chuẩn pattern rather than UG's favorites-only cache.

Audio analysis is where expectations must be set. Open-source chord estimation has plateaued: Chordino reaches about 80% chord-symbol recall, autochord (25 major/minor classes) scores 67.3% on Billboard, the field "still has not exceeded 90% accuracy on simple chord label prediction tasks," 2025 conformer and Mamba models describe a "glass ceiling" partly caused by annotator subjectivity, extended-chord accuracy drops sharply, and a December 2025 thesis finds performance "does not increase with model size past a low threshold" ([ISMIR 2015](https://www.ismir2015.uma.es/articles/271_Paper.pdf); [ISMIR 2021 LBD](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/latebreaking/000008.pdf); [arXiv 2509.01588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01588v1); [arXiv 2512.22621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22621v1)). An LLM post-processing pass (GPT-4o over MIR outputs) adds only 1 to 2.77% on the MIREX metric ([arXiv 2509.18700](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.18700v1)). Stem separation is strong (HT-Demucs about 7.5 dB SDR on MUSDB18-HQ, 9.0 with extra data), monophonic pitch via CREPE is solved, and Basic Pitch handles polyphonic note transcription one instrument at a time ([SCNet paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.13276); [PyPI basic-pitch](https://pypi.org/project/basic-pitch/0.3.1)). A pragmatic pipeline is Demucs stems, beat tracking, a chord model on the no-drums stem restricted to major and minor triads, optional LLM key-consistency cleanup, WhisperX or syncalong lyric alignment only if lyrics are licensed, and a human correction UI; expect 65 to 80% raw triad accuracy and treat "chords from any song" as a draft generator for community correction, not a transcription product. Real-time mic detection has no maintained browser library; chroma plus template matching in an AudioWorklet or a small on-device classifier makes a "practice helper," which is also all that UG's Practice Mode is. Klangio is the one vendor found selling chord and strum recognition with on-device licensing if buying beats building ([Oomol Klangio](https://oomol.com/es/skills/oo-klangio/source.md)).

SEO works differently for chords than for lyrics. "Let it be chords" draws about 38,100 searches per month as an informational query, and Google does not answer chord queries in the SERP ([Semrush e-chords](https://cw.semrush.com/website/e-chords.com/overview)); lyrics queries, by contrast, are answered on the results page with licensed lyrics, and Google's rater guidelines scored third-party lyrics sites only "Moderately Meeting" intent ([House Judiciary exhibit](https://docs.house.gov/meetings/JU/JU05/20190716/109793/HHRG-116-JU05-20190716-SD008.pdf)). Google's March 2024 scaled content abuse policy targets "large amounts of unoriginal content that provides little to no value to users, no matter how it's created," so programmatic chord pages must each carry real data (key, capo, diagrams, difficulty, strumming pattern, timestamps, ratings) and never be auto-generated from unreviewed model output ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies)). Schema.org MusicComposition with lyricist and inLanguage is valid markup, but no Google documentation treats it as a ranking signal ([schema.org lyrics](https://auto.schema.org/lyrics)). Khmer script, with no spaces between words and multiple romanization variants, is a search-and-lyrics engineering problem that local sites would need to solve and UG has not; it is a small, real moat.

## Conclusion

The teardown changes the question. UG is not vulnerable on catalog scale, Western licensing or SEO authority, and a solo founder should not try to beat it there; UG's exposed flank is the gap between what it must do to recover royalty costs (gate, upsell, auto-renew) and what its users say they want (transparent pricing, a clean subscriber experience, beginner rhythm help, worship tooling, offline access, local repertoire). The same licensing debt that forces UG's paywall creep does not exist for a chords-first product in languages where UG holds no licensed catalog, and the Cambodian payment rails that forbid silent auto-renewal happen to enforce the pricing honesty UG's class action is about. The strategic sequence is therefore the inverse of UG's history: start where rights are simplest (chords, community charts, Khmer repertoire with direct label relationships), earn trust with monthly or prepaid pricing, and add licensed lyrics and English catalog only once revenue covers the aggregator minimums. The largest open unknown is demand itself: no Cambodian musician was quoted in any source and no Khmer traffic data exists, so the first investment should be twenty conversations with Khmer guitarists, church worship teams and teachers, not code.

## Where a new entrant can win

The following advantages are concrete, each tied to evidence in the preceding sections. They are ordered by how directly the evidence supports them.

1. **Own Khmer repertoire outright.** No Khmer chord website surfaced in any query; the only two dedicated apps (Khmer Chords, Khmer Worship) were iOS-only, last updated February 17, 2023, and are delisted, and UG hosts no Khmer catalog ([appshunter](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/khmer-chords/id1630776488)). A curated Khmer chord library with Khmer-script search, romanization matching and capo suggestions for singers has no competitor, living or dead, and the prior apps' existence proves demand.

2. **Launch Android and web first with the whole catalog offline.** UG's Indonesian traffic is 84% mobile, its offline mode covers only pre-downloaded favorites that must be re-downloaded after reinstall, backing tracks cannot be saved, and 61% of its complaint reports come from Android ([Semrush](https://cw.semrush.com/website/ultimate-guitar.com/overview); [UG Help: offline](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/7936845-mobile-offline-access-to-your-favorites-how-to-add-load-and-troubleshoot); [unitQ](https://unitq.com/unitq-scorecards/ultimateguitar)). Hợp âm chuẩn's full-catalog offline sync with incremental updates is the proven regional model ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1057211347)); both Khmer predecessors died as iOS-only apps.

3. **Price transparently, monthly or prepaid, with no trial-to-annual trap.** UG's most common one-star theme is the 7-day trial attached to a $99 annual renewal, no automatic refund, and a cancel flow that must reach a final confirmation; it is the subject of a pending federal class action ([Truth in Advertising](https://truthinadvertising.org/class-action/ultimate-guitar/); [UG Help: cancel](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/6741667-how-to-cancel-your-ultimate-guitar-subscription)). KHQR has no recurring billing anyway, so a pay-per-period design with reminders and 3, 6 and 12-month prepay turns a constraint into the differentiator ([CartDNA](https://www.cartdna.com/en_GB/shopify-payment-methods/aba-payments)). Regional norms (Hợp âm chuẩn $1.99 one-time, U-FRET ¥650 per month) set the price band for SEA; a separate, higher English-market tier can run on Stripe through a foreign entity.

4. **Sell a subscriber experience with zero upsells.** Paying UG subscribers report "aggressive ad walls every time I try and do anything," and Trustpilot's summary names "frustration with ads, even for paying subscribers" ([JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/357828853/ultimate-guitar-chords-tabs/reviews); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.ultimate-guitar.com)). A plain promise that paid means no promotions of any kind is cheap to deliver and directly converts the roughly 20% of users complaining about interruption frequency.

5. **Keep transpose, Simplify, autoscroll and print free forever.** These are the features reviewers most often cite as "always free, and now, suddenly, they aren't" ([App Store ZA](https://apps.apple.com/za/app/id357828853)). They cost nothing to provide on community charts (ChordSheetJS does transposition client-side), and committing to them publicly draws a line UG cannot match without unwinding its royalty recovery model.

6. **Ship strumming patterns and simplified-chord layers on every song.** Beginners say strumming patterns are "only occasionally given" and UG pages carry "there is no strumming pattern for this song yet" ([JustinGuitar community](https://community.justinguitar.com/t/strumming-patterns/79747); [UG tab](https://tabs.ultimate-guitar.com/tab/maryzark/hindi-na-chords-498596)); Chordtela became Indonesia's #1 music site by positioning on "basic chords, easy guitar keys" ([Mojok](https://mojok.co/terminal/chordtela-adalah-solusi-bagi-orang-yang-skill-gitarannya-pas-pasan-kayak-saya/)). A required strumming field, D/U arrows above lyric lines and a one-tap "easy version" toggle address the most-voiced learner frustration.

7. **Build the worship stack UG does not have.** UG offers no Nashville numbers, setlists, one-tap key change or team sync; worship leaders retrofit a browser extension and pay for OnSong (Apple-only, $59.99 per year), Planning Center Music Stand (about $10 per month) or use the unpolished free WorshipTools ([KANTAN Chord](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/android/addon/instachordizer/); [learnofchrist OnSong](https://learnofchrist.com/resources/onsong); [CRCNA](https://network.crcna.org/topic/worship/general-worship/what-are-pros-and-cons-using-planning-center-online)). Khmer Worship existed and was delisted, so the Cambodian church segment is both demonstrated and vacant. Chords plus numbers plus setlists plus offline, priced for SEA congregations, has no incumbent.

8. **Fill the teacher-student hole left by Tonara.** Tonara shut down December 8, 2023; the remaining tools are either studio admin (MyMusicStaff) or notation-centric (Soundslice), and none assign chord charts or log practice at the low end ([Wikipedia: Tonara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonara_(company)); [Soundslice](https://www.soundslice.com/blog/214/introducing-practice-lists/)). A teacher mode (assign a chart, see practice days) is a small addition to a chord app and a distinct B2B2C channel.

9. **Carry less licensing debt by starting chords-first.** Bare chord progressions are generally regarded as unprotectable, which is the foundation Chordify built on; UG must pay royalties on its licensed layer and therefore gates it, and it says so in its own App Store replies ([Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2014/03/10/chordify/); [App Store NO](https://apps.apple.com/no/app/id357828853)). A product that shows chords, user-contributed charts under a real notice-and-takedown process, and licensed lyrics only after revenue clears the aggregator minimum (about $25,000 per year in the dated 2015 figure) can keep its free tier stable instead of shrinking it. This is a risk-management observation, not legal advice.

10. **Make audio-to-chords a community draft tool, not a paywalled oracle.** UG's May 2026 AI chords is Pro-gated, app-only and slow ("10 minutes or less"), and open-source chord recognition tops out around 67 to 80% on triads ([UG Help: AI chords](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/14771579-mobile-what-are-the-ai-chords); [ISMIR 2021 LBD](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/latebreaking/000008.pdf)). For local-language songs that nobody has charted, a free Demucs-plus-chord-model draft that contributors correct is how a thin catalog grows fast, and the corrected charts become the SEO pages that the scaled-content policy rewards ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies)).

11. **Win the small-phone and accessibility segments with a clean, labelled UI.** Reviewers call UG's phone interface "crowded," "cluttered" with "small text," and a blind user says it "has become more inaccessible now" yet stays because it is "the only chord service I have found" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/websites-apps/ultimate-guitar); [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/redesigning-the-ultimate-guitar-app-dd74915ed9c9); [AppleVis](https://applevis.com/comment/83360)). A text-first chart on mid-range Android with large type and labelled controls is table stakes in SEA and a loyal niche globally.

12. **Capture SEO where UG is thin and Google does not compete.** Chord queries are high-volume and unanswered in the SERP, UG's Thai and Hindi content is sparse user-generated material, and Thailand and Vietnam are outside its top five countries ([Semrush e-chords](https://cw.semrush.com/website/e-chords.com/overview); [Semrush UG](https://cw.semrush.com/website/ultimate-guitar.com/overview)). Khmer, Thai, Vietnamese, Indonesian and Tagalog "song + chords" long tail, each page carrying real data, is open ground; lyrics-only pages are not worth building because Google answers them itself.

13. **Use the lower 2026 platform fees and a web-first checkout to out-margin UG's Paddle era.** Google Play now charges 10% on subscriptions plus 5% only if its billing is used, Apple's Small Business Program is 15% then 10% on renewals, and web checkout costs under 5%; UG built its web funnel specifically to dodge 15 to 30% store fees ([Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/google-starts-cutting-play-store-fees-to-a-flat-10-service-fee-on-the-first-1m-and-allows-external-payment-links-making-good-on-its-epic-games-settlement/); [Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/); [Paddle](https://www.paddle.com/studios/shows/fix-that-funnel/ultimate-guitar)). A new entrant starts with those economics without the discount theatre that generates UG's complaint volume.
