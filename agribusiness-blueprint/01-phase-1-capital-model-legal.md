## 1.3 Low-Risk Entry Mechanics

### Pillar 1: Start reselling with $450, not a brand

**What to sell first:** small-pack, high-value fertilizers (foliar liquids, bio-organic, micronutrients, crop-specific blends for durian, pepper, cassava, vegetables). Do not start with 50 kg urea or DAP bags: margin per bag is thin, shipping is heavy, and farmers buy those from the shop in their own village.

**What not to sell yet:** pesticides, herbicides, fungicides. You need a MAFF/PDAFF agricultural materials business license first (see 1.6, Stage 3). Until then, pesticide questions get referred to one licensed partner dealer, and you ask that dealer for a 5% referral fee in writing.

**How the $450 works:**

1. Pick **one** existing MAFF-registered brand with 3 SKUs (the P1.1 to P1.3 lines). Huy Yun is one candidate from the video; compare it against at least two others in Phase 2. Do not commit until you have seen the MAFF registration number on every label.
2. Ask for **dealer pricing at the $200 to $500 tier** plus one of: 30-day payment on 50% of the order, buy-back of unopened stock within 60 days, or free sample units for the test plot.
3. Ask whether the supplier can **drop-ship** to provincial buyers via bus companies (they pack, you sell, you pay them on each order). If yes, keep only fast movers at home and drop-ship the rest. This cuts your inventory cash by about half.
4. Run the **test plot** (P1.4): same crop, two side-by-side rows, one with the product per label instructions, one with the farmer's normal practice. Film on Day 0, 14, 28 and at harvest. This becomes your strongest ad asset and costs $60.
5. Track reorders. Fertilizer is a repeat purchase every crop cycle. The OEM decision is based on repeat buyers, not one-off sales.

### Pillar 2: Consignment and small-MOQ deals

Cambodian processors are often strong at production and weak at selling, which is your leverage. Offer them three deal shapes and let them pick:

| Deal shape | How it works | When to offer |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Split payment** | You pay 50% on pickup, 50% within 14 days. | Default first offer. Shows you are serious. |
| **B. Consignment (ផ្ញើលក់)** | You take 20 to 30 units, pay only for sold units every 14 days, return unsold units within 60 days in sealed condition. | When the processor has stock sitting and trusts you after a visit. |
| **C. Small-MOQ cash** | You buy 20 units outright at a slightly higher price. | When they refuse consignment. |

Rules that protect you: buy from **3 different production dates** to check consistency; never take more than 60 days of estimated sales; insist on production and expiry dates printed on each pouch; keep the processor's original label until you are registered (relabeling food under your own brand triggers your own registration obligations).

### Pillar 3: Zero-inventory pre-orders

You never hold fruit. The sequence each week:

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| Monday | Post "This week's harvest drop" with photos/video from the farmer (sent via Telegram). Price per kg, minimum 3 kg. |
| Mon to Wed | Take orders in Messenger/Telegram. Customer pays a **50% deposit** by ABA KHQR or Wing. Phnom Penh repeat customers can do COD after their first paid order. |
| Wednesday 8 PM | Close the list. Send the farmer the exact kg count. You pay the farmer from the P3.5 float. |
| Thursday | Farmer harvests and packs per your grading standard; ships by bus (Virak Buntham, Capitol, or the farmer's usual bus company). |
| Friday/Saturday | You receive in Phnom Penh, inspect, deliver via local delivery app or customer pickup; provincial customers receive by bus parcel. Collect balances. |
| Sunday | Repay the float from customer balances. Post unboxing and customer photos. |

**Commission vs buy-and-resell:** for the first 4 weeks, use commission (you collect the customer's money, keep 15%, send 85% to the farmer) if the farmer agrees. It removes your stock risk entirely. Move to buy-and-resell once you know your weekly volume and want a better margin.

**Season reality (Nov 2026):** durian is out of season. Sell **Battambang oranges and pomelo** now to build the buyer list, and use Dec to Apr to sign durian orchards for the 2027 harvest (Script B in Phase 2). By the time durian arrives, you will have a few hundred fruit buyers already in your Telegram channel.

## 1.4 OEM / Private-Label Trigger ("the $5,000 threshold")

The locked $980 (P1.6) is released only when **all five** are true:

1. Cumulative Pillar 1 sales revenue of **$5,000 or more**.
2. At least **40 repeat buyers** (bought fertilizer 2+ times).
3. Reseller gross margin held at **25% or more** for 3 consecutive months.
4. You have completed **Stage 1 and Stage 2** registration (section 1.6).
5. You have a written OEM quote from at least **two** manufacturers covering MOQ, unit cost, who holds the MAFF product registration, and lead time.

If the OEM quote requires more than the $980 plus 50% of accumulated Pillar 1 profit, you wait. Do not dip into the reserve for OEM.

## 1.6 Cambodian Legal & Compliance Roadmap

**Disclaimer:** fees, thresholds and procedures below change. Confirm on the official Ministry of Commerce (MoC) online business registration portal, with the General Department of Taxation (GDT), and with your Provincial Department of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (PDAFF) before filing. A licensed tax agent typically handles Stages 1 and 2 for a fee; budget from OPS.2.

### Stage 0: Informal online seller (Day 1 to registration trigger)

What you do:

1. Sell under a Facebook Page and Telegram channel; receive money in the dedicated ABA account.
2. Keep every supplier invoice and every sale in the ledger. This is your evidence for tax registration later and protects you if a customer disputes a product.
3. Sell **only MAFF-registered fertilizers** with the registration number on the label. Never sell imported inputs with foreign-only labels (a common sign of unregistered or smuggled product).
4. Sell processed food **in the processor's original labeled packaging**.
5. No pesticides. No health claims like "cures diabetes" on banana flour or lotus powder. Say "traditionally used," "rich in fiber," and only what the processor's label or a lab test supports.

Honest note: Cambodia's tax and e-commerce rules technically apply to all business activity, and enforcement on small online sellers varies. Stage 0 is meant to be short, a test period, not a permanent status.

### Stage 6: MAFF registration of your private-label fertilizer (start at OEM trigger; allow 6 to 12 months EST)

| Step | What happens | Who does it |
|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | Decide the registration holder: **(a)** the OEM manufacturer registers the product with your brand name and you are the exclusive distributor, or **(b)** your company registers it as the brand owner with the manufacturer named. | 6.2 | Submit application with product composition, lab analysis from an approved laboratory, Khmer label draft (product name, nutrient content, usage, safety, net weight, manufacturer, registration number field, batch, expiry). | Holder |

### Later stages (Phase 5 and 6 detail)

Export of processed food to the US, Australia, France and South Korea requires additional steps (manufacturer food facility requirements in the destination country, English/destination-language labeling, allergen declaration for cashews, certificate of origin via MoC, and destination import rules; Australia's biosecurity rules are strict on nuts and plant products). These are covered fully in Phase 5. Do not promise overseas customers delivery until that workflow is set up.
