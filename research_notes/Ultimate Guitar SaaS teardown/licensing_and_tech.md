# Licensing and Technical Requirements for a Chord/Tab/Lyrics Subscription SaaS (2026), Cambodia-based founder, SEA + global English

> Scope note: These are research notes compiled on 2026-10-09 for a report writer. Nothing here is formal legal advice; copyright and platform-fee rules are in flux (Apple's US external-link commission is still before a court; several SEA copyright amendments are drafts). Several primary pages (Google Play Console help, Wikipedia, chordify.net FAQ, lyricfind.com, developer.payway.com.kh, musicradar) were blocked by the research proxy, so some findings rest on secondary coverage; those are flagged.

## Key Question 1: Are chord charts, tabs and lyrics protected works, and what minimum licensing does a startup need to display chords + lyrics in the US/EU? What do small startups actually do?

### Takeaway
Lyrics are unambiguously protected literary/musical-work content and require a publisher (or aggregator such as LyricFind/Musixmatch) license to display; tabs and chord-plus-lyric sheets are widely treated by publishers (MPA/NMPA) and most legal commentators as unlicensed derivative works, although no US court has squarely ruled on tabs and some commentators argue fair use. Bare chord progressions on their own are generally regarded as unprotectable, which is the legal foundation Chordify built on before it added licensed lyrics. In practice small entrants either (a) show chords only with no lyrics, (b) run user-generated content under DMCA safe harbor with a registered agent and takedown process, or (c) license lyrics through an aggregator once they have revenue.

### Cited Findings

**Chords, tabs and derivative works**
- Music publishers (MPA and NMPA) argued in 2006 that tab sites and transcribers violate the copyright holder's exclusive "right to make and distribute arrangements, adaptations, abridgements, or transcriptions of copyrighted musical works," and that "tablature postings, even inaccurate ones" are protected derivative works; sites were told they could face legal action or ISP pressure, and "All of the sites have taken down their tabs voluntarily, but grudgingly." — [Arizona Law Review, Jason Simon note (50 Ariz. L. Rev. 611)](https://arizonalawreview.org/pdf/50-2/50arizlrev611.pdf); [Slashdot 2007 coverage](https://news.slashdot.org/story/07/05/15/2149224/threat-to-free-legal-guitar-tablature-online)
- The On-line Guitar Archive (OLGA) received a takedown letter from NMPA/MPA lawyers in 2006 and removed roughly 34,000 tablatures. — [Wikipedia: On-line Guitar Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-line_Guitar_Archive)
- MXtabs.net closed due to MPA legal threats, after which Ultimate Guitar saw a surge of users; UG argued it was not subject to MPA actions because it was headquartered in Russia and complied with Russian law. — [Wikipedia: Ultimate Guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Guitar) (full page blocked by proxy; relying on search-surfaced text)
- Public Knowledge's view: the publishers' derivative-work argument "is probably correct" because tabs are "nothing more than a translation of sounds into musical notation," like translating a French book into English; and because tab sites are commercial and may substitute for sheet music sales, "fair use is unlikely to protect the sites and their users." — [Public Knowledge](https://publicknowledge.org/?p=7791)
- A Vanderbilt J. of Entertainment & Technology Law note (2020) concludes guitar tabs are infringing derivative works but protected by fair use, and notes "a lack of legal precedent for copyright infringement in guitar tabs." — [Vanderbilt JETLaw vol. 9 iss. 3](https://scholarship.law.vanderbilt.edu/jetlaw/vol9/iss3/8)
- The Simon (Arizona) note concludes tablatures are "not fair use of copyright under current U.S. law" and proposes a compulsory license. — [Arizona Law Review](https://arizonalawreview.org/pdf/50-2/50arizlrev611.pdf)
- A William & Mary Law Review note finds tabs "can be, but are not usually, transformative." — [W&M Law Review (Kempema)](https://wmlawreview.org/sites/default/files/KEMPEMA49-6.pdf)
- Jonathan Zittrain (Harvard) was quoted in 2006 press suggesting user-generated tabs might "fit fair use, or would be found not to be a derivative work to begin with." — quoted in [Mathew Ingram's blog](https://mathewingram.com/work/?p=471)
- In Genius v. Google (2d Cir. 2022), the appeals court reasoned that Genius's lyric transcriptions are "derivative works" of the songwriter's copyright and Genius lacked standing because it did not own the lyrics; the Supreme Court declined review. — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/judge-throws-out-genius-lawsuit-accusing-google-of-copying-lyrics/); [9to5Google 2022](https://9to5google.com/2022/03/14/google-genius-lyric-lawsuit/); [The Playground](https://theplayground.co.uk/lyrics-website-genius-lawsuit-against-google-struck-down-by-supreme-court/)

**Bare chord progressions**
- Chordify's stated position (2014): "Unlike lyrics or melody, chord progressions are not innovative and sufficiently unique to be copyrighted on their own," and it removes individual songs on request of the copyright owner. — [Digital Music News, 2014](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2014/03/10/chordify/)
- General songwriting-law commentary: chord progressions on their own are not protected; melody, lyrics and a written arrangement are. — [Secrets of Songwriting](https://www.secretsofsongwriting.com/2009/01/09/what-does-copyright-really-protect/)
- Chordify later added lyrics via "a new strategic partnership with LyricFind," so Chordify now shows licensed lyrics rather than avoiding them entirely. — [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/chordify-lyrics-live-chord-detection) (page blocked by proxy; based on search-surfaced text)

**Licensing paths that incumbents use**
- Ultimate Guitar has signed license agreements with "thousands of publishers, including Sony, EMI, Peermusic, Alfred, Hal Leonard, Faber and Music Sales," under which songwriters are compensated for tab display; on April 10, 2010 UG signed an additional licensing agreement with the Harry Fox Agency. — [Wikipedia: Ultimate Guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Guitar)
- Muse Group (owner of Ultimate Guitar, MuseScore, Audacity, StaffPad) acquired Hal Leonard; the deal closed December 1, 2023, with growth investment from Francisco Partners; Hal Leonard's exclusively licensed arrangements (Taylor Swift, The Beatles, Disney, John Williams) are being digitized onto Muse platforms; combined library "over 5.5 million scores, tabs, books, video courses, backing tracks"; UG + MuseScore draw 300 million annual visitors and 40+ million accounts. — [Scoring Notes](https://www.scoringnotes.com/news/muse-group-acquires-hal-leonard/); [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/music-creation-company-muse-group-acquires-sheet-music-publisher-hal-leonard-with-backing-from-private-equity-firm-francisco-partners); [Complete Music Update](https://completemusicupdate.com/musegroup-acquires-sheet-music-publisher-hal-leonard-with-francisco-partners-backed-deal/)
- Songsterr's publisher royalties historically ran through Musicnotes (2015 Musicnotes press release counts "royalties related to the www.songsterr.com guitar tab business"). — [Musicnotes press release PDF](https://m.musicnotes.com/PressMaterials/Musicnotes%20Nears%20$60%20Million%20in%20Royalties%20Paid;%20Celebrates%205%20Millionth%20Customer.pdf)
- Musicnotes (a licensed sheet music retailer) had paid nearly $60 million in royalties as of 2015. — same source
- Jamzone (a licensed chords+lyrics app) notes that "despite our agreements with major music publishers, certain right holders can withdraw consent and we are required to not showcase chords and lyrics for some artists." — [Jamzone Substack comments](https://jamzone.substack.com/p/pro-guide-recording-exporting-and/comments)
- Google states "We license lyrics on Google Search from third parties, and we do not crawl or scrape websites to source lyrics"; LyricFind admitted it may have "unknowingly" sourced lyrics copied from Genius. — [9to5Google 2020](https://9to5google.com/2020/08/11/google-genius-lyric-lawsuit/)

**DMCA safe harbor mechanics (US)**
- The DMCA safe harbor for sites that host user-posted material requires designating an agent with the US Copyright Office; the fee is $6 per filing (down from $105), designations must be renewed every three years, and a lapse "will result in a lapse of the OSP's safe harbor protection until amendment or renewal occurs." — [US Copyright Office NewsNet 2016](https://copyright.gov/newsnet/2016/640.html); [Quarles](https://www.quarles.com/newsroom/publications/new-online-system-under-the-digital-millennium-copyright-act-requires-website-operators-to-re-register-agent-designations-by-2018); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/client-advisories/u-s-copyright-office-announces-important-changes-to-dmca-agent-designation.md)
- The safe harbor also requires adopting and implementing a repeat-infringer termination policy. — [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/want-to-keep-your-dmca-safe-harbor-new-76629/)

### Inferences
- The lowest-risk zero-license MVP is "chords only, no lyrics, no melodic tab," because bare chord progressions are the one element even publishers' critics concede is unprotectable; adding lyrics (even partial lines as cue words) is where a publisher license becomes necessary. This mirrors Chordify's original model.
- Note-for-note guitar tab (Songsterr/Guitar Pro style) is the riskiest unlicensed content category since it reproduces the melody/arrangement; the MPA's 2006 letters targeted exactly this.
- UG's history shows the viable path for a non-US startup: operate under a jurisdiction with weaker enforcement, grow to scale, then convert to publisher licensing. That path is far harder in 2026 for a Cambodia-based founder because Cambodia joined Berne in March 2022 (see Q6) and app stores/Google impose their own takedown regimes regardless of jurisdiction.
- The DMCA safe harbor only covers US liability; it does not shield the startup from EU or SEA claims, and it requires a genuine notice-and-takedown operation, not just a form.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve Chordify's current FAQ/terms (chordify.net blocked) to confirm its present stance on chord copyright or whether it pays publishers for chords.
- No source found on the terms (rate per view, minimums) of UG's publisher or HFA deals.
- No 2024–2026 litigation or MPA statements on tab sites were found; the question of tabs as derivative works remains untested in court as of the sources found.

## Key Question 2: What do LyricFind or Musixmatch API licenses cost for a startup?

### Takeaway
Neither aggregator publishes pricing; the only concrete numbers found are a 2015 Musixmatch quote of a USD 25,000 minimum annual data license and a per-display rate of not less than USD 1.40 CPM (per 1,000 lyric views). LyricFind states pricing is usage-based and requires direct contact. Treat these as dated floor estimates only.

### Cited Findings
- Musixmatch statement quoted in a 2015 Audacious bug tracker: "Annual fees range from USD $25,000 to higher depending on the audience for the data and which data are being licensed. We are not able to offer any sort of data license for less than USD $25,000"; and "For web/mobile usage they charge per CPM, meaning 1,000 lyrics viewed by end user, for not less than 1.4 USD CPM." — [Audacious Redmine issue #502](https://redmine.audacious-media-player.org/issues/502)
- A third-party AI-tools directory says LyricFind pricing "requires direct contact, potentially opaque" with no public free tier. — [neura.market LyricFind listing](https://www.neura.market/ai-tools-directory/new-ai-tools/lyricfind) (low-authority source)
- LyricFind's CEO in a 2016 interview: services include real-time lyrics delivery via XML/JSON or data feeds, "it's all based on usage," and royalties are paid based on the number of times a lyric is viewed. — [LyricFind press clipping](https://lyricfind.rockpaperscissors.biz/artist/8830/press_clips/10258/white)
- An unverified 2025 comparison blog claims Musixmatch API "tiered pricing starting at $0.006 per 1,000 queries" with free test credits; this is a query rate, not a display license, and could not be confirmed against Musixmatch. — [cdn.dji.nl blog](https://cdn.dji.nl/social-clips/song-lyrics-api-2025-1770408847) (low-authority)
- Musixmatch raised $3.7M in 2011 for its lyrics API and positioned itself as making online lyrics legal. — [neunetz.com 2011](https://neunetz.com/2011/05/18/musixmatch-erhalt-37-millionen-us-dollar-fur-songtext-api/); [TechCrunch 2010](https://techcrunch.com/2010/07/29/musixmatch-aims-to-make-online-lyrics-legal-with-a-new-e400k-round)
- Chordify Premium is priced roughly $8/month or $3.50/month on annual (basic $4/month or $2/month annual) per a 2026 review; app store trackers show yearly Premium at $54. — [Guitar Chalk Chordify review](https://www.guitarchalk.com/chordify-review/); [App Pricing Lab](https://apppricinglab.com/iap/apple/1073624757)
- Ultimate Guitar Pro US App Store: $9.99/month or $59.99/year (Pro, Courses, Sing bundle); Germany lists UG Pro+ 1 year at 39.99 EUR. — [Apple App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/app/apple-store/id357828853); [Apple App Store DE](https://apps.apple.com/DE/app/id357828853)
- Songsterr Plus subscription: $9.99/month in the US App Store. — [Apple App Store KR listing](https://apps.apple.com/KR/app/id399211291)

### Inferences
- At USD 1.40 CPM, 1 million monthly lyric views would cost roughly USD 1,400/month in royalties, which is affordable once a product has traction; the binding constraint is the USD 25k annual minimum (if still applied) and the sales process, not the per-view rate.
- A pragmatic sequence used by incumbents: launch chords-only, then add lyrics via an aggregator once revenue supports the minimum. Chordify did exactly this.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch developer.musixmatch.com/plans or lyricfind.com (blocked). No 2024–2026 pricing data exists publicly; all figures are 2015-era.
- Unknown whether either aggregator will license a Cambodia-registered entity directly, or whether SEA-language (Khmer, Thai, Vietnamese) repertoire coverage is adequate.

## Key Question 3: What rights are needed for backing tracks, YouTube embeds, and audio-to-chord analysis of user uploads?

### Takeaway
Embedding a YouTube video via the IFrame API is generally permitted under YouTube's API Terms with restrictions on overlays, player modification, autoplay and heavy ad-driven embedding; extracting audio from YouTube is prohibited by its terms. Backing tracks require mechanical (and for streaming, performance) licenses on the composition plus a master license if a recording is used. Audio-to-chord analysis of user-uploaded audio produces a transient reproduction of the recording and an (arguably) derivative chord chart; sources found do not resolve this directly, and the practical risk is low only if the audio is not stored/redistributed and the output is chords-only.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube's API "Required Minimum Functionality" rules: API clients using embedded players must adhere to size constraints, autoplay rules, and "restrictions on overlays or modifications to the player's appearance"; IFrame Player API clients must identify via the HTTP Referer header. — [Google Developers: YouTube API Services Required Minimum Functionality](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/required-minimum-functionality)
- A law-firm summary of YouTube guidance: occasional embedding on a blog with general ads is fine, but "websites that simply embed a large number of YouTube videos and aim solely to earn advertising revenue are considered to be in violation of the terms of service." — [Monolith Law Office](https://monolith.law/en/internet/youtube-terms-of-service-important-points) (secondary, Japan-based firm)
- Embedding by itself is generally not copyright infringement, but the underlying composition and chord transcription carry separate rights. — same source
- Chordify's product is built on extracting chords from YouTube/SoundCloud/Deezer audio and it removes songs on rights-holder request. — [Digital Music News 2014](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2014/03/10/chordify/)
- Moises (a licensed stem/chord app) offers AI chord detection in real time, unlimited for Premium/Pro, free accounts limited to the first minute, chords shown alongside lyrics, no chord export. — [Moises help center: chord detection](https://help.moises.ai/hc/articles/6569274648220-How-do-I-use-Chord-Detection); [Moises help: export](https://help.moises.ai/hc/en-us/articles/17065260809116-Can-I-Export-Chords-or-Sheet-Music-from-the-App)
- Thailand's Department of Intellectual Property warned in August 2026 that using protected songs with AI for voice conversion, remixes or generation may require licenses and royalties to multiple rights holders, and that copying "when lyrics, melody, musical composition or other substantial parts stay recognisable" can be infringement absent an exception. — [Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/business/tech/40069742)
- Vietnam's 2022 IP Law amendments (effective Jan 1, 2023) explicitly permit "temporary reproductions of works during technological processes" under certain conditions. — [Mondaq: Vietnam IP Law amendments 2022](https://mondaq.com/trademark/1498960/vietnam-ip-law-amendments-2022-what-businesses-need-to-know)

### Inferences
- A sync feature should drive off the IFrame player's playback-time events (getCurrentTime polling) rather than touching the audio stream; that keeps the startup inside YouTube's terms and avoids "downloading" the content. Overlaying chord diagrams on top of the player element itself is a terms risk; render chords beside the player.
- Backing tracks for original or user-generated recordings of public-domain or self-written songs are license-free; for commercial songs, a mechanical/performance license is needed and a Cambodia-based entity has no obvious local route to one (see Q6), so most startups avoid hosting backing tracks of copyrighted songs.
- For user-uploaded audio analysis, a defensible design is: process in memory or short-lived storage, delete the upload after analysis, never redistribute the audio, output chords only (no lyrics transcription unless licensed), and require users to warrant they have rights to the audio.

### Gaps
- No primary source on how Chordify or Moises license (or do not license) the analysis of user-uploaded copyrighted audio; no court decision found on whether machine-generated chord charts from a recording are derivative works.
- Could not retrieve the current YouTube API Services Terms of Service text itself.

## Key Question 4: Copyright and collecting societies in Cambodia, Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia, Philippines

### Takeaway
Cambodia's 2003 Law on Copyright and Related Rights governs, Cambodia joined the Berne Convention effective March 9, 2022 (so foreign works are now protected there), a 2016 Prakas created a legal basis for CMOs, and copyright amendments were being consulted on in September 2025; I could not identify an operating Cambodian music CMO by name. The five main SEA societies (FILSCAP, WAMI, MACP, MCT, VCPMC) formed AAMRO with SESAC/MINT in 2023 for multi-territory digital licensing; Thailand has the clearest DMCA-style safe harbor (2022 amendment), the Philippines relies on administrative site-blocking plus platform liability, Indonesia on the ITE Law and a 2023 Constitutional Court ruling, and Vietnam on blocking orders.

### Cited Findings

**Cambodia**
- Main statute: Law on Copyrights and Related Rights (2003), enacted as part of WTO accession; "work" covers the literary, scientific, artistic or musical domain. — [NCDD library: 2003 Copyright Law (English)](https://lib.ncdd.gov.kh/storage/app/public/library_backend/CAT_6489_1/2003-Copyright%20Law%20english.pdf); [AIPA Lync](https://aipalync.org/document/law-on-copyrights-and-related-rights); [Khurana & Khurana](https://www.khuranaandkhurana.com/?p=12372)
- Berne: instrument deposited December 9, 2021; in force March 9, 2022 (Berne Notification No. 287); Cambodia declared it is not bound by Art. 33(1) and invoked the Appendix developing-country provisions for ten years expiring October 2024. — [WIPO Berne Notification 287](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/es/treaties/notifications/details/treaty_berne_287); [WIPO Lex Cambodia treaties](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/treaties/ShowResults?code=KH); [WIPO DG news 2021](https://www.wipo.int/about-wipo/en/dg_tang/news/2021/news_0069.html)
- Before accession, the Cambodian law "does not offer automatic protection to foreign works"; accession was expected to extend protection to foreign works. — [Tilleke & Gibbins 2020](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/cambodia-accede-berne-convention-greatly-expanding-foreign-copyright-owners-rights/?page=2)
- An older IIPA Special 301 report (2007) described Cambodia as a TRIPS member but not a Berne member; superseded by the 2022 accession. — [IIPA 2007 Cambodia](https://www.iipa.org/files/uploads/2018/01/rbc20072007SPEC301CAMBODIA.pdf)
- CMO framework: Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts Prakas No. 112 PR.K on Collective Management Organizations, July 5, 2016, defining a CMO as the representative of authors, composers and rights holders. — [WIPO Lex text 544320](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/544320); [WIPO Lex legislation 19476](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/fr/legislation/details/19476)
- A 2011 WIPO technical assistance entry noted a national CMO was still being planned. — [WIPO TAD activity 1756](https://www.wipo.int/tad/en/activitydetails.jsp?id=1756)
- March 2024: PM Hun Manet asked the Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts to strengthen IP and copyright enforcement and update the legal framework for technology. — [Asia IP Law](https://asiaiplaw.com/section/cover-story/cambodia-pm-urges-ministry-to-fortify-efforts-to-ip-rights)
- September 19, 2025: the Ministry and the Bar Association held a workshop on proposed amendments to the Law on Copyright and Related Rights to respond to technological change and align with international treaties. — [EU IP Helpdesk](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/ministry-culture-gathers-input-copyright-law-amendments-2025-09-20_en)
- Term: a blog argues Sinn Sisamouth's compositions enter the public domain in 2026 under the life+50 term in Article 30, and mentions a single collecting society; unverified. — [Interspace Music blog](https://interspacemusic.com/blog/?p=19083) (low authority)
- A 2012 Commons summary lists Cambodia's accession dates; one secondary source still wrongly says Cambodia is not a Berne member. — [Wikimedia Commons CRT/Cambodia](https://c.enwp.org/wiki/Commons:CRT/Cambodia); contradicted by older [ssrana.in](https://ssrana.in/global-ip/international-copyright/copyrights-in-cambodia/)

**Regional societies**
- In July 2023 FILSCAP (Philippines), WAMI (Indonesia), MACP (Malaysia), MCT (Thailand) and VCPMC (Vietnam) formed the Asian Alliance Music Rights Organization (AAMRO), headquartered in Liechtenstein, administered by MINT Digital Services (SESAC + SUISA), for multi-territorial digital licensing in Europe, Africa and the Middle East. — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/sesac-partners-with-five-southeast-asian-collection-societies-to-form-multi-territorial-digital-licensing-hub/); [RightsTech](https://rightstech.com/2023/07/sesac-partners-with-five-southeast-asian-collection-societies-to-form-licensing-hub/); [Complete Music Update](https://completemusicupdate.com/five-asian-collecting-societies-partner-with-sesac-and-mint-on-digital-licensing-hub/)
- FILSCAP is accredited by IPOPHL as a CMO, has about 1,900 members and reciprocal agreements with 50 foreign societies, licenses public performance, mechanical reproduction and synchronization; e.g., concerts pay 2.25% of net ticket sales; FILSCAP states playing one song without a license is already infringement (2026 compliance drive). — [FILSCAP about](https://filscap.org/about-us/); [FILSCAP licensing](https://filscap.org/licensing/); [PhilStar June 30, 2026](https://www.philstar.com/cebu-business/2026/06/30/2538773/filscap-pushes-music-licensing-compliance); [Wikipedia FILSCAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Society_of_Composers,_Authors_and_Publishers)
- Thailand has around 30 music collecting societies, so users may need to deal with several; IIPA 2020 said Thailand should introduce CMO governance rules. — [Tilleke "Let the Music Play" 2016 PDF](https://www.tilleke.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/2016_Jul_Let_the_Music_Play.pdf); [IIPA 2020 Thailand](https://www.iipa.org/files/uploads/2020/02/2020SPEC301THAILAND.pdf)
- Thai IP&IT Court precedent treats the lyricist and the sound-recording owner as separate rights holders. — [Asia IP Law](https://www.asiaiplaw.com/section/ip-analysts/repetition-of-copyright-royalty-collection-in-sound-recordings-and-musical-work)

**Safe harbor / takedown regimes**
- Thailand's 2022 Copyright Act amendment replaced the court-dependent Section 32/3 mechanism with a notice-and-takedown regime classifying ISPs as intermediary, caching, hosting and search engine providers; removal duties apply to caching, hosting and search providers, which must offer accessible reporting channels. — [Tilleke & Gibbins 2022 amendment analysis](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/the-2022-amendment-of-thailands-copyright-act-the-good-the-bad-and-the-backstory); [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/report/the-2022-amendment-of-thailands-copyright-act-the/)
- Philippines: IPOPHL Memorandum Circular 2023-025 (voluntary administrative site blocking, in effect January 14, 2024); the Internet Transactions Act (RA 11967) imposes subsidiary liability on platforms that fail to promptly remove infringing content after a government takedown order; IPOPHL has proposed IP Code amendments granting takedown powers (status unconfirmed, 2025). — [Rouse: content and site blocking in SE Asia](https://rouse.com/insights/news/2024/content-and-site-blocking-in-se-asia); [Malaya Sept 2025](https://malaya.com.ph/banner/law-to-avert-piracy-losses)
- Indonesia: Copyright Law 28/2014 has no specific platform-liability provisions; the ITE Law requires ISPs to remove content after notices; Constitutional Court Decision 84/PUU-XXI/2023 (brought by music companies) extended Article 10 liability to UGC platforms; a draft amendment would introduce a structured notice-and-takedown with safe harbour (enactment unconfirmed). — [Mondaq: Indonesia updates copyright law](https://www.mondaq.com/copyright/1806078/indonesia-updates-copyright-law); [UNDIP journal study](https://ejournal.undip.ac.id/index.php/mmh/article/view/77878)
- Vietnam: the IP Law places responsibility on ISPs to block infringing content after valid reports; the Authority of Broadcasting and Electronic Information executes blocking orders; the 2022 amendments (effective Jan 1, 2023) revised 100+ articles. — [Rouse SE Asia site blocking](https://rouse.com/insights/news/2024/content-and-site-blocking-in-se-asia); [ASEAN Briefing](https://www.aseanbriefing.com/news/vietnams-intellectual-property-law-new-amendments/); [Asia IP Law: Vietnam IP law ratified](https://asiaiplaw.com/article/vietnams-amended-intellectual-property-law-ratified)

### Inferences
- Because Cambodia is now in Berne, Western publishers' works are protected in Cambodia; "hosted in Cambodia" is not a shield the way "hosted in Russia" was for UG in 2006. The practical enforcement risk in Cambodia itself is still low, but the app stores, Google Search, and payment processors are where takedowns actually bite.
- For Khmer, Thai, Vietnamese, Indonesian and Filipino repertoire there is no equivalent of LyricFind with deep coverage; direct deals with local labels/publishers (which in SEA often own both master and publishing) and with the national societies are the realistic route for lyrics in local languages.
- The SEA societies primarily license performance/mechanical/sync; none of the sources describe a lyric-display or "graphic rights" tariff, so a lyric-display license for local repertoire will likely be a bespoke negotiation.

### Gaps
- No source names an operating Cambodian music CMO ("CamboSA" could not be verified at all).
- No tariff or process found for MCT (Thailand), WAMI (Indonesia) or VCPMC (Vietnam) regarding lyric reproduction/display online.
- Enactment status of the Indonesian notice-and-takedown amendment and the Philippine IP Code amendments is unconfirmed as of these sources.

## Key Question 5: Reference architecture — formats, rendering libraries, tab playback, audio analysis, on-device detection, LLM cleanup, YouTube sync; and how accurate is open-source chord recognition?

### Takeaway
A mature open-source stack exists for every layer except real-time browser chord detection: ChordSheetJS (ChordPro parse/format/transpose), SVGuitar (MIT, SVG chord diagrams), alphaTab (MPL-2.0, Guitar Pro 3–7/MusicXML/alphaTex rendering plus alphaSynth playback), Demucs (stem separation), Basic Pitch (note transcription), WhisperX/syncalong (lyric forced alignment). Open-source chord recognizers land around 67% (autochord, 25-class maj/min) to roughly 80% (MIREX best on Beatles/Queen datasets) and the field has plateaued; a 2025 paper shows GPT-4o post-processing adds 1–2.77% on MIREX metrics. "Good enough" is reached by restricting vocabulary to major/minor triads, beat-synchronizing, and letting users correct.

### Cited Findings

**Sheet format and rendering**
- ChordSheetJS: JavaScript library for parsing (ChordPro and plain text), formatting (HTML, text) and transposing chord sheets; converts chords to numeric/Nashville; GPL-3.0 per a mirror listing; current npm version 15.x. — [GitHub bettermusic/ChordSheetJS](https://github.com/bettermusic/ChordSheetJS); [npm README v15.2.0](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/chordsheetjs@15.2.0/README.md); [SourceForge mirror](https://www.sourceforge.net/mirror/chordsheetjs)
- ChordFiddle: online ChordPro playground built on ChordSheetJS + ChordJS with whole-sheet or selection transposition. — [GitHub martijnversluis/chordfiddle](https://github.com/martijnversluis/chordfiddle)
- chorpro-parser: TypeScript ChordPro parser with Transposer and ChordProFormatter. — [GitHub chordproject/chorpro-parser](https://github.com/chordproject/chorpro-parser)
- SVGuitar: MIT-licensed JS/TS library that "Create[s] beautiful SVG guitar chord charts"; supports fingers, barres, open/muted strings, titles, fret labels, orientation, left-handed mode, string count, tuning labels, colors; works in browser or Node (headless via toSvg()); published as `svguitar` and `@svguitar/core`. — [GitHub omnibrain/svguitar](https://github.com/omnibrain/svguitar)
- Obsidian "Leadsheet" plugin illustrates the feature set users expect: transpose, capo shapes, chord diagrams, set lists, autoscroll. — [Obsidian Stats: Leadsheet](https://www.obsidianstats.com/plugins/leadsheet)

**Tab playback**
- alphaTab: MPL-2.0; inputs Guitar Pro 3–5, 6, 7, alphaTex, MusicXML (basic); SVG or raster output; platforms: browser (script/ESM), Node, .NET Standard 2.0/.NET 8, Android/Kotlin; built-in MIDI + SoundFont2 synthesizer "alphaSynth" using Web Audio. Guitar Pro 8 and React/Vue bindings are not listed on the README. — [GitHub CoderLine/alphaTab](https://github.com/CoderLine/alphaTab); [alphaTab 1.0 release notes](https://alphatab.net/docs/releases/release1_0)

**Audio analysis pipeline**
- autochord: Python library; NNLS-Chroma VAMP plugin features into a Bi-LSTM-CRF (TensorFlow); 25 classes (12 maj, 12 min, no-chord); chord accuracy 67.33% on 100 Billboard songs, root WCSR 74.77, maj/min WCSR 70.62; 0.11 s inference for a 4-minute song on CPU; Python 3.6/Ubuntu era; TF.js port blocked because CRF ops are unsupported. — [PyPI autochord](https://pypi.org/project/autochord/0.1.3); [ISMIR 2021 late-breaking paper](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/latebreaking/000008.pdf)
- MIREX chord estimation: best reported results have reached 80.22% on a Beatles/Queen/Zweieck collection (earlier years: 74% in 2009, later 81%). — [Academia: Using Online Chord Databases](https://www.academia.edu/18862243/Using_Online_Chord_Databases_to_Enhance_Chord_Recognition); [core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/works/8853160)
- December 2025 thesis (Mackenzie): "progress in automatic chord recognition has been slow since the advent of deep learning," classifiers perform poorly on rare chords, pitch augmentation helps, performance "does not increase with model size past a low threshold," likely due to ambiguous annotations and rare chord classes. — [arXiv 2512.22621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22621v1)
- September 2025 "ChordCoT" paper: uses HT-Demucs stems (drum-removed, drum-and-vocal-removed, bass) as inputs to a large-vocabulary recognizer, then GPT-4o chain-of-thought post-processing, giving "overall accuracy gains of 1-2.77% on the MIREX metric"; code at github.com/WildHoneyPie/ChordCoT. — [arXiv 2509.18700](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.18700v1)
- Basic Pitch (Spotify): lightweight multi-pitch/note/pitch-bend transcriber; "note accuracy competes with much larger and more resource-hungry AMT systems"; polyphonic but "works best on one instrument at a time"; outputs notes, not chords. — [PyPI basic-pitch 0.3.1](https://pypi.org/project/basic-pitch/0.3.1); [IRCAM paper page](https://brahms.ircam.fr/fr/media/x6cb24a_basic-pitch-a-lightweight-model-for-multi)
- A Hugging Face Space chains Demucs stems into Basic Pitch for multi-track MIDI (notes "use a beefy Space or local env"). — [HF Space mrblackdev/Voice-To-MiDI-VTM](https://huggingface.co/spaces/mrblackdev/Voice-To-MiDI-VTM/blob/main/app.py)
- "amadeus-chordzart" documents an Audio → Note Detection (Basic Pitch) → Chord Assembly → Post-Processing pipeline and claims "~75% on pop/rock" with no dataset or method given; unreliable. — [amadeus-chordzart docs](https://amadeus-chordzart.readthedocs.io/en/stable/pipeline/chord_detection.html)
- A convolutional-transformer ACR model for extended chord qualities was presented at SBCM (Brazilian Symposium on Computer Music). — [SBCM paper](https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/sbcm/article/view/43948)

**Real-time on-device detection**
- No maintained single library combines Web Audio + TF.js + chroma for browser chord detection; `chord-recognition` (npm) is a TypeScript port of Stark & Plumbley's real-time algorithm, self-described as "not meant to be efficient"; `bluesharp-pitch-detection` ships an AudioWorklet pitch processor with a chord example; CREPE runs via TensorFlow.js for pitch. — [npm chord-recognition](https://npmjs.com/package/chord-recognition); [bluesharp-pitch-detection example](https://app.unpkg.com/bluesharp-pitch-detection@3.4.3/files/npm/examples/index.html); [autochord roadmap](https://pypi.org/project/autochord/0.1.3)
- A Chrome extension "Real-time Chord Recognition for Browser Audio (YouTube, Spotify and Others)" exists, showing the approach is feasible client-side. — [Chrome Web Store listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/eoffiogmiehknnnimmbaablfkceeagmb)

**Lyric alignment and LLM cleanup**
- WhisperX: faster-whisper transcription + wav2vec2 word-level forced alignment + pyannote diarization; Interspeech 2023 (arXiv 2303.00747); plain Whisper timestamps are utterance-level and can be off by seconds; limitations: unmappable characters (numbers) get no timing, language-specific wav2vec2 model needed, overlapping voices handled poorly. — [PyPI whisperx](https://pypi.org/project/whisperx); [Fora Soft guide 2026](https://www.forasoft.com/learn/ai-for-video-engineering/articles-ai/whisperx-diarization-word-level-timestamps)
- syncalong: forced alignment of known plain-text lyrics to audio, exported as timestamped LRC, using Whisper word timestamps plus a dynamic-programming aligner; CLI and Python library. — [syncalong docs](https://syncalong.readthedocs.io/)
- LLM-as-post-processor for chords is the ChordCoT approach above (GPT-4o over MIR tool outputs). — [arXiv 2509.18700](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.18700v1)

**App stack**
- Expo SDK 57 with React Native 0.86 shipped June 30, 2026 with zero breaking changes; React Native repo ~126k stars vs Flutter ~178k. — [pasqualepillitteri.it React Native 2026](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/8279/react-native-2026-stack-choices-vs-flutter)

If you know Dart, Flutter"; recommends Supabase + EAS + RevenueCat + Sentry; cites RevenueCat's State of Subscription Apps 2026 (115,000+ apps; top quartile grew 80% YoY, bottom quartile shrank 33%). — [Expo blog (RevenueCat DA author)](https://expo.dev/blog/best-way-to-build-a-mobile-app-that-makes-money-in-2026)
- A 2026 security study found AI coding agents often generate Supabase tables without Row-Level Security enabled. — [pkgpulse mobile guides](https://www.pkgpulse.com/guides/tag/mobile) (secondary)

### Inferences (reference architecture)
- Data model: store songs as ChordPro source (canonical), with precomputed HTML and a transposition offset applied client-side via ChordSheetJS; chord diagrams generated on demand from a chord-name-to-voicing table rendered by SVGuitar; autoscroll is a trivial requestAnimationFrame loop keyed to a user BPM setting.
- Tab tier: ingest Guitar Pro files and render/play with alphaTab (MPL-2.0 allows commercial use with file-level copyleft); note this is the highest-risk content category legally (Q1).
- Analysis pipeline (server, GPU optional): upload → Demucs/HT-Demucs stems → beat tracking (madmom or equivalent) → chroma + chord model (autochord-class or a transformer ACR) on the no-drums stem → optional LLM pass to enforce key consistency and simplify to a maj/min vocabulary → WhisperX/syncalong lyric alignment only if lyrics are licensed → human/user correction UI. Expect ~65–80% raw chord accuracy on triads; extended chords will be unreliable.
- Real-time mic detection: implement chroma (FFT in an AudioWorklet) + template matching for maj/min triads in-browser; or on mobile, a small TFLite chroma classifier. Accept that it is a "practice helper," not a transcription tool.
- YouTube sync: store per-song chord timestamps, poll `player.getCurrentTime()` from the IFrame API and highlight the active chord; never pull audio.

## Key Question 6: Costs and limits of Apple/Google in-app purchase in 2026; can web checkout avoid fees?

### Takeaway
Apple: standard 30%, Small Business Program 15% (10% for subscriptions after year one / EU alternative terms) on proceeds up to USD 1M; in the US, since the April 2025 contempt ruling Apple may not collect commission on external-link purchases, and in August 2026 Apple proposed 15% standard / 10% subscriptions-renewals-and-partner-programs / 5% Small Business Program for linked-out purchases, which Epic is contesting (decision pending as of October 2026). Google Play: from June 30, 2026 in the US/UK/EEA, a 10% service fee on the first USD 1M per year and on subscriptions (reports differ on higher tiers of 15–20% above that), plus a 5% billing fee only if Google Play Billing is used; external links/alternative billing avoid the billing fee but not the service fee. Web checkout (Stripe) is fully fee-free of store commissions when the purchase happens outside the app and the app does not steer in disallowed ways.

## Key Question 8: SEO — how chord pages rank, programmatic SEO risk, schema.org markup, Google's treatment of lyrics pages

### Takeaway
Chord queries are high-volume informational queries ("let it be chords" ~38,100/month) and incumbents get roughly half their traffic from Google organic; Google answers lyrics queries on the SERP with licensed lyrics (LyricFind etc.) and rates third-party lyrics sites only "Moderately Meeting," so lyrics pages are structurally disadvantaged while chord pages are not (Google does not show chords in SERPs). schema.org MusicComposition/MusicRecording with `lyrics`/`lyricist` is valid but no Google documentation treats it as a ranking signal. Since March 2024, Google's scaled content abuse policy targets mass-produced low-value pages regardless of method, so programmatic chord pages must carry real unique data (chords, key, capo, difficulty, timestamps).

### Cited Findings
- Semrush estimates ultimate-guitar.com at ~68.35M monthly visits with Google organic ~48% and direct ~41%; smaller chord sites (chords-and-tabs.net ~60% organic). — [Semrush ultimate-guitar.com overview (DE)](https://de.semrush.com/website/ultimate-guitar.com/overview/); [Semrush chords-and-tabs.net](https://www.semrush.com/website/chords-and-tabs.net/overview/)
- Semrush keyword data for e-chords.com tags "let it be chords" as informational intent with 38,100 monthly searches. — [Semrush e-chords.com](https://cw.semrush.com/website/e-chords.com/overview)
- Google began showing full licensed lyrics in search in 2014–2016 (tied to Google Play catalog and licensing deals) and lyrics sites saw sharp visibility drops; Rap Genius was penalized for link schemes but restored within ten days. — [Search Engine Land 2014](https://searchengineland.com/report-2014-bad-year-lyrics-sites-google-213156); [TechCrunch 2014](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1097671)
- Google's search quality rater guidelines (per a 2019 House Judiciary exhibit) instructed raters to score lyrics websites such as Genius as "Moderately Meeting" intent while Google's own lyrics boxes rated highest. — [House Judiciary exhibit SD008](https://docs.house.gov/meetings/JU/JU05/20190716/109793/HHRG-116-JU05-20190716-SD008.pdf)
- Google added attribution of licensed lyrics providers in search after the Genius dispute. — [Search Engine Land lyrics topic](https://searchengineland.com/topic/lyrics); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/author/matt-southern/page/100/)
- Music answer boxes/lyrics boxes trigger on music queries and reduce click-through. — [Go Fish Digital](https://gofishdigital.com/blog/music-lyrics-ads-answer-boxes-google/)
- schema.org: `lyrics` (expects CreativeWork) and `lyricist` (Person) are properties of MusicComposition; vocabulary developed with MusicBrainz. — [schema.org lyrics](https://auto.schema.org/lyrics); [schema.org lyricist](https://google.schema.org/lyricist)
- Google's March 5, 2024 update introduced scaled content abuse ("creating large amounts of unoriginal content that provides little to no value to users, no matter how it's created"), site reputation abuse (manual actions from May 6, 2024) and expired domain abuse; Google reported ~40–45% reduction in low-quality unoriginal content. — [Google Search Central blog, March 2024](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies); [Siteimprove on site reputation abuse](https://www.siteimprove.com/blog/understand-googles-site-reputation-abuse-policy/)
- Commentators note programmatic SEO is acceptable when each page delivers original data, context or evidence; volume alone is not the violation. — [Victorious: scaled content abuse](https://victorious.com/blog/scaled-content-abuse/)
- ISMIR 2013 research found search ranking of lyrics pages reflects links/clicks rather than lyric accuracy. — [AMiner: Ranking lyrics for online search](https://www.aminer.org/pub/53e9ad6cb7602d970375659e/ranking-lyrics-for-online-search)

### Inferences
- A chord site's SEO moat is "song + chords" long-tail at scale with genuinely useful per-page data (key, capo, chord diagrams, difficulty, strumming pattern, video sync, transposition, user ratings), plus local-language coverage (Khmer/Thai/Vietnamese/Indonesian/Tagalog song chords) where incumbents are thin. Lyrics-only pages are a poor SEO bet because Google answers them in-SERP.
- Use MusicComposition (composer, lyricist, iswcCode if known, inLanguage, musicalKey if using the Google-extended vocabulary) plus MusicRecording/VideoObject for the embedded video, and BreadcrumbList; expect schema to aid entity understanding, not rankings.
- Avoid auto-generating thousands of chord pages from unreviewed ML output without a quality gate; that pattern is exactly what the scaled content abuse policy describes.

### Gaps
- No Google documentation found on how (or whether) chord pages specifically are treated; no evidence of chord sites being deindexed.
- Google's current (2025–2026) spam policy page text was not fetched directly; one secondary source claims a "site reputation policy" rename and a 2026 EU enforcement date, unconfirmed.
