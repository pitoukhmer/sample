### Paperwork for a formal import (to confirm with a broker)

Reported requirements: company registered with the Ministry of Commerce, VAT registration with the General Department of Taxation, then registration with GDCE; customs import declaration (ASYCUDA); commercial invoice, packing list, transport document; certificate of origin if claiming a preferential rate; permits from line ministries for regulated goods. Sources: [US Commercial Service, import requirements](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/cambodia-import-requirements-documentation) · [GDCE Manual on Customs Procedures](https://tools.customs.gov.kh/documents/reader/17709/17373560374410226/Manual%20on%20Customs%20Procedures%20in%20Cambodia%20%28English%29). Whether a sole proprietor below the VAT threshold can import commercially in their own name: **not found; ask the broker.**

## 6. Landed cost formula

```
Goods value (EXW or FOB)          = unit price x quantity
+ China inland + export handling  (zero if price is FOB or the agent includes it)
+ International freight
+ Insurance
= CIF value

Customs duty   = CIF x CD rate
Special tax    = (CIF + Customs duty) x ST rate            (0 if no ST applies)
Import VAT     = (CIF + Customs duty + Special tax) x 10%
+ Broker fee, port/terminal handling, inspection, standards/label costs
+ Trucking Phnom Penh to the shop
+ Bank transfer and currency conversion cost
+ Sourcing agent commission, if any
+ Allowance for dead-on-arrival units = total x expected defect rate (no supplier swap on direct imports)
= Total landed cost

Landed cost per unit = Total landed cost / number of SALEABLE units
```

Import VAT is only recoverable if the business is VAT-registered and the declaration is in its name.

While buying from the Phnom Penh wholesaler, these obligations sit with whoever imported the goods. On a direct import they become the founder's. That transfer of liability is a cost of stage 3 that does not show in the landed-cost table.

**Documents to ask a Chinese factory for** (my checklist; the Cambodian rules above reference IEC standards, so IEC-based reports are the relevant ones):

1. CB Scheme test certificate and report to IEC 60335-1 plus the part 2 for the product (2-15 kettles and liquid heaters, 2-14 kitchen machines/blenders, 2-9 grills and air fryers, 2-8 clippers and shavers, 2-23 hair care, 2-80 fans). Check that the model number and factory name on the report match the goods.
2. China CCC certificate for the same model, as evidence the factory is audited (it is a Chinese domestic mark, not accepted as such in Cambodia).
3. Food-contact test report (LFGB or FDA type) for jugs, pots, baskets and non-stick coatings.
4. For battery products: UN38.3 test summary and MSDS. Forwarders will ask for these.
5. For chargers and adaptors: separate test report; this is where the recorded failures are (section 3).
6. Business licence of the factory and, for branded goods, a letter showing the right to sell the brand.

A "CE" logo on a box is a self-declaration and proves nothing by itself; ask for the test report behind it.

**When it becomes worth it. This is reasoning, not a sourced threshold:**

- At the MOQs found, one private-label SKU means holding roughly 500 units. With a $10,000 stock cap and a two-week turn target, that is only sane for a product already selling about 250 or more units a month, so that one MOQ is about two months of sales. No product in a new shop meets that test; revisit after six months of own sales data.
- Order of steps that keeps risk low: (1) sell the wholesaler's version and record monthly units and fault rate per model; (2) buy one carton of the candidate factory's standard model through a consolidator and run it beside the existing brand; (3) only then order with own logo and Khmer manual; (4) register the mark in Cambodia before the goods ship.
- First candidates should be low-fault, no-battery, low-liability items where the cost of a bad batch is small. Kettles also have the reported 0% duty. Leave air fryers, heating blenders and anything with a lithium cell until the testing bench has a track record.
- With a private label the founder is the brand: standards registration, warranty and any injury claim are his. Budget for a test report check and a pre-shipment inspection on every order.
