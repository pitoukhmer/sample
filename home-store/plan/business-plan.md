# Phteah Yeung Home (working name): Business Plan v1

Version 1, 28 Nov 2026. Built from the founder's photos, videos and answers plus public sources. It has not yet been merged with the ChatGPT, Kimi and Gemini research (see `../README.md`). Tags used: [FOUNDER DATA], [SOURCE], [ASSUMPTION]. Every assumption is listed in `assumptions.md` with a way to check it.

## A. Verdict

Worth starting, small, as a test with a hard review at day 60. The setup is unusually low risk: no rent, supplier credit of 15 days, supplier delivery to the door, and a full time owner-operator [FOUNDER DATA]. The weak point is margin. Everyone can buy the same boxes, so the gross margin may be thin, and the model shows that thin margin leaves little after one wage.

What the model says with placeholder inputs (all [ASSUMPTION], see `financial-model.xlsx`): at an 18 percent gross margin and about $385 of monthly fixed costs, the shop must sell about $2,100 a month, roughly $76 per open day, before it earns anything. Below that it loses money. This is a break-even calculation and not an income forecast.

Three ways this fails:
1. Margin is lower than hoped because Phnom Penh sellers and other local shops price the same brands near wholesale.
2. Stock that does not sell. The wholesaler does not take unsold goods back [FOUNDER DATA].
3. The commune is small, about 12,400 people in 2019 [SOURCE: citypopulation.de], so walk-in demand has a ceiling and online must add to it.

Best starting wedge: be the place near the market where a family or a vendor can see an item working, buy it today, and bring it back within 7 days if it is faulty. Start with daily and urgent items, add demonstration items through live video.

## C. Product selection

Full scored table of 42 products in `product-scoring.csv`. Scores are the planner's judgment on a 1 to 5 scale, not measured data, and margin is left blank until the wholesale list arrives. Local walk-in fit and demand frequency carry the most weight.

Launch range, about 22 items:
- Traffic items (bought often, bring people in): LED bulbs, lunch boxes, bottles and tumblers, extension cords, mosquito rackets, stainless basins.
- Core household: rice cooker, kettle, fan, wok or pan, vacuum flask, iron, portable gas stove.
- Vendor and trade: price computing scale, ice crusher, hair clippers.
- Demonstration items for live video, one or two units each: table blender, heating blender, 3 in 1 mixer, air fryer, electric frying pan.
- Buy to order only: folding beds beyond two on display, slow juicer, food machines.

Several top-scoring items (rice cookers, fans, extension cords, rechargeable lights) were not visible in the wholesaler footage. Confirm the wholesaler carries them or find a second supplier.

Do not sell at launch: Philips or any global brand without proof of authorised supply; phone chargers and power strips from unknown makers (fire risk); gas canisters until storage rules are checked; anything described by a country of origin that paperwork does not prove.

## D. Unit economics

Use the `UnitEconomics` sheet. For each product enter wholesale cost, share of restock delivery, packaging and any delivery subsidy. The sheet returns the minimum selling price for the target margin and the real margin at the chosen price.

Minimum price = (cost + restock share + packaging + delivery subsidy) / (1 - warranty reserve - target margin).

Rule: if the local or online competitor price is below the minimum price, do not stock the item. Offer it to order only, or skip it.

## E. Sourcing and restocking

Stage 1, months 1 to 3: the one Phnom Penh wholesaler, weekly delivered orders, 15 day credit. Stage 2, months 3 to 6: a second wholesaler for the gaps and for price comparison; ask for better terms on the 20 best sellers once volumes are known. Stage 3, after month 6 and only if 3 to 5 items sell steadily: direct import or private label, costed with the Kimi research and a licensed customs broker.

Restocking rules (full version in the research brief, section 2d):
1. One fixed order day a week, top-ups only for sold-out items.
2. Class A fast sellers hold up to two weeks of sales. Class B holds one on display and one boxed. Class C is bought to order with a deposit.
3. Buy the opening stock in two halves, two weeks apart.
4. Never owe the supplier more than about two weeks of sales, and no more than $3,000 in the first three months [ASSUMPTION]. Pay on day 14 from sales.
5. No sale in 30 days: cut the price or bundle it, and do not reorder.
6. Test every electrical unit on the day it arrives so faults fall inside the 7 day swap.

Opening stock: $4,000 to $5,000 planned, first half about $2,000 to $2,500 [ASSUMPTION]. The model suggests two weeks of cover needs well under the $10,000 cap at early sales levels, so resist filling the room.

## F. Shop design (7.7 m x 12.5 m)

Suggested zones, front to back. Measure door and window positions before fixing this.

| Zone | Depth | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Front 3 m | full width | Open display facing the street: traffic items, bundles, price signs in riel and dollars, counter and cash drawer by the door |
| Middle 5 m | left wall and centre shelves | Appliances by use: cooking, cooling, lighting, personal care. One unboxed display unit each |
| Middle, right wall | about 2 m wide | Pitou Foods corner, own banner, own shelf, away from gas stoves and cleaning goods |
| Rear 2.5 m | left | Testing bench with sockets, water, a rice and fruit supply for demos; doubles as the live video set with fixed lighting and a phone stand |
| Rear 2 m | right | Packing table, courier pickup shelf, boxed stock on labelled racks |

Fit-out budget $800 to $1,200 [ASSUMPTION]: second-hand steel racks, sign, bench, lights, phone tripod, extension reels with breakers, fire extinguisher. Get two local quotes.

Roles: the owner runs buying, pricing, cash, live selling and customer chat. The assistant runs the floor, testing, packing and stock counts. Keep Pitou Foods sales in a separate column of the daily sheet and separate stock count. No Halal wording or symbols anywhere on the Pitou Foods corner.

Security: cash banked or moved daily, a camera over the counter and door, serial number noted on each sale for warranty.

## G. Channels, in order

1. The shop itself and its Google Maps listing. Free and immediate.
2. One Facebook page with Messenger, plus a Telegram channel for regular buyers. Facebook is the main social commerce channel in Cambodia [SOURCE: trade.gov].
3. Short product videos posted to Facebook and TikTok from the same recordings.
4. Facebook live from the test bench twice a week once there are at least 15 products to show.
5. Khmer24 listings for the higher priced items.
6. Delay: TikTok Shop, a website, paid ads beyond a small test. Add them when orders show which products travel.

## H. Content system

Three recordings a day while unpacking and testing, each under 40 seconds: the item working, one honest fact (wattage, capacity, what it cannot do), the price, and the swap promise. Weekly pattern: Monday new arrivals, Wednesday vendor tools, Friday household bundle, Sunday live. Ready-to-use Khmer and English replies are in `../ops/reply-scripts.md`. A native speaker must read the Khmer before use.

## I. Fulfilment and after sales

Local: hand over after testing in front of the buyer. Delivery within the commune by motorbike for a small fee or free above a set basket [ASSUMPTION].
Online: test on the bench, film 10 seconds, send the clip to the buyer, pack with corner protection, hand to a courier. Courier choice and rates are not confirmed for Khsach Kandal; get rate cards from at least three couriers and ask about COD cash return time.
Prefer KHQR payment or a deposit for buyers far away; COD refusals cost two courier trips.
Warranty the business can afford is in `../ops/warranty-policy.md`: 7 day swap on everything, longer only where the wholesaler confirms it in writing.

Being ahead here means doing the dull things reliably. None of these is a secret; most sellers skip them.

## M. First 90 days

| Week | Do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Get the wholesale price list, delivery fee, minimum order and swap terms in writing. Walk the local market and two nearby shops; note their prices for 20 items. Check the name and register the page handles |
| 2 | Fill the UnitEconomics sheet. Choose the launch range. Fit out the room. Get courier rate cards. Confirm registration steps |
| 3 | First half of opening stock. Test everything. Price labels. Soft opening. Start daily counts |
| 4 | Google Maps listing, Facebook page, Telegram channel. First 20 videos. Hire the assistant |
| 5 to 6 | Second half of opening stock, only items that moved. First live. Visit vendors and barbers in person with the Telegram QR |
| 7 to 8 | Day 60 review against the kill criteria below. Drop dead items. Merge in the other AI research and the critic's findings |
| 9 to 12 | Add pre-order service online, bundles, second supplier quotes. Decide on TikTok Shop and paid ads from real data |

Kill and continue criteria at day 60 [ASSUMPTION, adjust once margin is known]: continue if sales per open day are at or above the break-even figure from the model with real inputs, or clearly climbing toward it week on week. If sales are under half of break-even with no upward trend, stop buying new lines, sell down stock, and keep only the items that turn.

Eight weekly numbers: sales walk-in and online, gross margin, stock value at cost, amount owed to the supplier, class A stock-outs, faulty units, items 30 days unsold, repeat buyers.

## N. Risks

| Risk | Early sign | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Dead stock, no returns to supplier | Items past 30 days | Small first lots, 30 day rule, pre-order for slow lines |
| Thin margin | Local prices at or below the minimum price | Skip those items, sell bundles and accessories, second supplier |
| Counterfeit or unsafe goods | No documents, very low price, hot plugs or cables on test | Do not stock, keep the faulty log, drop the brand |
| Over-ordering on open credit | Owed amount above two weeks of sales | Own ceiling, pay on day 14 |
| COD refusals | Refusal rate above 5 percent | Deposit by KHQR for distant buyers, test clip before dispatch |
| Injury or fire from a product | Any customer report | Stop selling the model, tell the wholesaler, record it |
| Mixing Pitou Foods and appliance money | Cash does not reconcile | Separate columns and stock counts from day one |
| Owner overload | Messages unanswered for hours | Saved replies, assistant on the floor, fixed live schedule |
| Seasonal dips | Rainy season footfall | Shift to mosquito, lighting, indoor items and online |
